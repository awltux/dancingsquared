# How a call works

Every call in this book has the same three-part shape, and the manual states all three parts
explicitly. Once you can see the shape, a call you have never heard of becomes readable.

```
STARTING FORMATION  →  DANCE ACTION  →  ENDING FORMATION
```

## Starting formation

**Where you must already be standing for the call to make sense.**

This is the first thing to look at when a call goes wrong. Nine times out of ten the trouble is not
the movement — it is that you were not in the formation the call assumes. A call's starting
formation is not a suggestion; it is part of the call's definition.

Formations are explained in [Formations](../02-language/formations.md).

## Dance action

**What each dancer does.** The manual's definition of the action is deliberately precise, and it is
what this book quotes on every call page.

Most actions are described relative to where you are and which way you are facing — "the dancer
facing out walks in a 180-degree arc" — rather than in absolute compass directions. That is what
lets the same call work no matter which wall you are near.

## Ending formation

**The shape you are in when it is over.** Also part of the definition, and also worth checking when
something goes wrong: if you know what shape you should be in, you can often find your way there
even after losing track.

Some calls end in the same formation they started from, rotated. Some end in a different one
entirely. Some — like [Star Thru](../04-calls/m18-star-thru.md) — change a facing pair into a
couple.

## Timing

Every call has a **beat cost**. That number is how the caller fits calls to the music, and it is why
some calls feel much longer than others. See [Timing and music](timing-and-music.md).

## Where the definition is not enough

Some calls cannot be written as "do these steps from here". The manual says so, and handles them in
three ways you will meet:

- **Geometry calls** — the end state is defined by *identity* rather than by a path. **Promenade
  Home** is the standard example: you are not told which way to walk; the call is defined as
  finishing on your own home spot, facing in. If the board does not meet its precondition the call
  refuses rather than guessing.
- **Suffix calls** — the action depends on the call before it. **Sweep a Quarter** is one: it
  circles a quarter in whichever direction continues the motion that just finished.
- **Rules that adjust a call to where you are** — the [Facing Couples Rule](../03-rules/facing-couples-rule.md)
  and the [Ocean Wave Rule](../03-rules/ocean-wave-rule.md) let certain calls start from a formation
  slightly different from the one named.

## Why "where you are" is not "how it looks"

Square dancing describes positions and facings, not compass directions, because the caller can call
the same figure from any arrangement of the square. If you rotate the whole square — or reflect it —
a correct definition still works, and every dancer still does the same thing *relative to their own
situation*.

This is also why two dancers can be doing the same call and moving in opposite compass directions,
and both be right.
