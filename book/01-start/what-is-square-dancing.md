# What square dancing is

**Modern Western Square Dancing** is eight people, one caller, and a piece of music. The eight
dancers stand in a square. The caller names a movement — a *call* — and the dancers do it,
together, without being told in advance what is coming next. The caller then names another. And
another. The dance continues until the figures bring everyone back to where they started.

That is the whole idea. Everything else in this book is detail.

## What it is not

It is not a dance you learn as a fixed routine. There is no sequence to memorise, because the
caller decides the sequence live. What you memorise is a **vocabulary of calls** — around fifty of
them in the Mainstream program — and the skill of hearing one and doing it without thinking.

This is why square dancing is taught as a *language*. A call is a word. A formation is a
grammatical position. The conventions in [03-rules](../03-rules/passing-rule.md) are the grammar
that removes ambiguity when a word could mean two things.

## What an evening is like

An evening is a series of **tips** — see [Tips, the unit of the dance](tips-and-figures.md). Each
tip is a few minutes of dancing to one piece of music, and there are usually six to ten tips in a
night. Between tips you find a new square, and it does not matter who you get: the calls work from
any arrangement of the eight positions.

You will dance with strangers and friends, you will swap partners many times inside a single tip,
and you will finish the evening tired and, usually, laughing.

## Come as you are

Modern square dancing has **no dress code** for learning. Comfortable shoes and clothes you can
walk in are the whole requirement. Traditional dress exists in some clubs; ask before you turn up
in it.

Dancers are not required to bring a partner. Squares need eight, and clubs balance numbers as
people arrive.

## The 2026 change — important if you have danced before

If you are returning to square dancing, or reading older material, you need to know that the
program changed.

In 2025 CALLERLAB's membership voted to replace the previous **Basic**, **Mainstream** and **SSD**
programs with a single **Mainstream Dance Program** of **50 calls or call families**. The new
program, and the teaching order that goes with it, took effect on **1 September 2026**.

Two consequences you will notice:

- **Some calls you know are no longer Mainstream.** They have moved up. The Plus program — see
  [Where Mainstream leads](../07-plus/where-mainstream-leads.md) — absorbed some of them, and a few
  left the taught programs altogether. [Moved and renamed](../08-reference/moved-and-renamed.md)
  lists them.
- **The teaching order changed.** If you learned before 2026, the order you learned in is not the
  order in this book. The calls are largely the same ones; the route through them is not.
