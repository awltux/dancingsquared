# Zeroes, get-outs and modules

Three words callers use constantly, and the three ideas that explain why a square can be trusted to
come back together.

## In sequence, at home

When the eight of you are in your own positions and it is your turn again, the square is **at home
and in sequence**. This is the state a tip starts in and the state it has to end in.

The full story is in [Sequence and relationship](../02-language/sequence-and-relationship.md); the
short version is that there is a correct "order" around the square, and *in sequence* means you are
standing in it.

## A zero

A **zero** is a sequence of calls that "goes out and comes back": it leaves the squared set, travels
around, and returns everyone **to the same state** — same positions, same sequence, same
relationships.

> A tip is a zero. Every figure in it is usually a zero too.

Zeroes are why the caller can safely repeat a figure with the next couple leading: after a zero,
the square is back where it started, so the next couple can take over.

## A get-out

A **get-out** is a short sequence that takes a square that is *not* at home and brings it home —
back to the squared set, in sequence, ready to finish.

You will hear the last part of a tip described as the get-out. Its job is to leave the square in the
state a standard finish closes: promenade home, and the tip is done.

Get-outs are what a caller practises most, because the end of a tip is the hardest part to land
exactly: the music is running out and the square has to arrive home on the phrase.

## A module

A **module** is a short, named stretch of calls that behaves like a single call. Callers build them
from zeroes and get-outs and reuse them.

Two kinds you will meet:

- **Fix / get-out modules** — a saved sequence that rescues a square that has drifted off home.
- **Practice modules** — a short routine a teacher uses to drill a particular call in a real
  context, rather than on its own.

In the app, a module appears in the call picker alongside ordinary calls and can be applied like one.
That is the point: *a module is a call made of calls.*

## Why a beginner should care

Not because you need to build any — but because these three words explain the reassurance at the
centre of square dancing:

- the **zero** is why a tip can be repeated and chained;
- the **get-out** is why you never finish a tip lost;
- the **module** is why teachers can give you a short piece of real dancing to practise instead of a
  bare movement.

If you lose your place mid-figure, you are in a get-out waiting to happen. Keep dancing the calls
and someone will bring you home.
