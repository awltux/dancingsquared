# Fractions

Callers constantly specify **how far**: *Circle Left 3/4*, *Square Thru 2*, *Half Tag*. The
fractions are not vague — each has a defined meaning, and each has a timing.

## The fractions

| Fraction | Means |
|---|---|
| **1/4** | a quarter turn, or a quarter of the way round |
| **1/2** | half |
| **3/4** | three quarters |
| **Full** | all the way round |
| **1 1/2**, **2 1/2** | once and a half, twice and a half — met in [Square Thru](../04-calls/m21-square-thru.md) and in circulates |

The manual is explicit in one place that matters: for **Single Circle**, "the amount to move is
specified as a fraction of a full circle (**there is no default**)". So "Single Circle" alone is not
a complete command — you need the fraction.

## Fractions are of different things

This is the thing beginners find hardest, and it is not their fault: a fraction is always a fraction
of *something specific to that call*.

| Call | The fraction is of… |
|---|---|
| Circle Left 1/4 | the circle |
| Single Circle 1/2 | the circle of two |
| Turn Partner by the Right 1/2 | the arm turn |
| Square Thru 2 | the four hands of the square thru |
| Tag the Line 3/4 | the tag |
| Sweep a Quarter | a quarter circle, continuing the previous motion |

You do not have to work this out from first principles while dancing. The call page for each call
says what its fractions mean.

## Timing of fractions

Fractions have their own timings, and larger fractions are usually *less* than proportionally
longer — a half turn does not cost exactly twice a quarter turn, because you are already moving.

The manual's Circle Family gives a clear example:

| Circle Left | Beats |
|---|---|
| 1/4 | 4 |
| 1/2 | 8 |
| 3/4 | 12 |
| Full | 16 |

And the four-dancer circle, which is smaller and therefore quicker:

| Circle Four Left | Beats |
|---|---|
| 1/2 | 4 |
| 3/4 | 6 |
| Full | 8 |

Notice that the full four-dancer circle costs half of what the eight-dancer full circle costs. That
difference is the whole reason the manual states timings call by call.

## "And a quarter more"

A common suffix — *"Right and Left Thru **and a quarter more**"* — means: dance the call, then
**turn an additional quarter** in the flow of the call. It is a way of arriving in a different
facing without a second call.
