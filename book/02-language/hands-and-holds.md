# Hands and holds

Square dancing is danced **holding on**. Which hands you take, and how, is not decoration: several
calls are defined in terms of a particular handhold, and taking the wrong one makes the call
impossible.

## The holds in the Mainstream program

| Hold | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Couple handhold** | the ordinary side-by-side hold: your right hand joined with their right, or left with left, depending on the side you are on |
| **Normal couple handhold** | the couple handhold with palms matched — Boys' palms up, Girls' palms down in the classic styling |
| **Ocean Wave hold** | adjacent dancers joined by **right hands** (or **left hands**), arms relaxed, hands up |
| **Hands up** | both hands joined with adjacent dancers, palms up, elbows relaxed — the standard wave styling |
| **Forearm hold** | forearm-to-forearm, used for arm turns |
| **Palm star** | all hands into the middle, right hands stacked palm to palm (or left) |
| **Arches** | one pair's joined hands raised, as in [Star Thru](../04-calls/m18-star-thru.md) and [California Twirl](../04-calls/m27-california-twirl.md) |
| **Promenade hold** | the crossed-hand couple hold used in promenade — see [Promenade](../04-calls/m03-promenade-family.md) |

## A few rules about hands

**One partner per hand.** You never hold two dancers by the same hand.

**Hands are joined only where the call says so.** Between calls in a squared set, the customary
state is hands joined in a ring; in a line or wave, the hands the formation implies.

**Release deliberately.** Several calls — [Pass Thru](../04-calls/m07-pass-thru.md) is the clearest
— require you to **release** as you pass. Holding on turns a clean pass into a traffic accident.

**Take hands from the formation, not from habit.** A wave wants hands up; a couple wants a couple
handhold; an Alamo Ring wants everyone's hands in. Look at the shape and hold what it needs.

## Styling

The manual's styling sections are worth reading on each call page, because they describe what the
call should *look* like when it is danced well. Three general points from the manual's styling
chapter:

- **Posture** — stand up, weight on the balls of the feet, ready to move.
- **Hands and arms** — arms in a natural dance position when not joined; elbows comfortably bent,
  hands above the elbow in circles and waves.
- **Inactive dancers and counter-dancing** — dancers who are not named in a call must still **move
  out of the way** of those who are, and then take the next call. This is real dancing, not standing
  about, and it is expected of you.

## The one that catches everyone

The **arms in natural dance position** instruction is not a suggestion to drop your arms and relax.
It means arms relaxed but **ready** — the manual says so explicitly in several calls, because the
next call is usually already coming.
