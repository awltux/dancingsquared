# Facing Couples Rule

Some calls are written to start from an **Ocean Wave**, but dancers are often standing in **Facing
Couples** — the same four people, not yet stepped into a wave. The Facing Couples Rule bridges the
gap.

> "A few specific calls that are defined to start from an Ocean Wave are also proper starting from
> Facing Couples. Examples include Swing Thru and Spin the Top. In these cases, the dancers first
> **step into a momentary Right-Hand Ocean Wave** and complete the call."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

## What you do

1. **Step forward** into the wave the call expects — right hands joined, unless the caller directed a
   left-hand call.
2. **Dance the call** as written.
3. You finish in the wave the call ends in. You do **not** step back out.

For a left-hand call — *"Left Swing Thru"* — you step into a momentary **Left-Hand** wave instead.

## It is not a free pass

The rule applies **only** to calls whose definition says it does. The manual is blunt:

> "If the Facing Couples Rule is applicable, the call's definition in this document will have a
> comment to that effect. If there is no such comment, then the Facing Couples Rule **may not** be
> used."

At Mainstream, the calls whose definitions carry that comment include
[Swing Thru](../04-calls/m33-swing-thru.md) and [Spin the Top](../04-calls/m46-spin-the-top.md), and
their left-hand and grand variants.

## It is wider than its name

> "While the rule is called the Facing Couples Rule, it is generalized to include Facing Dancers
> stepping to a Right-Hand Mini-Wave and Facing Lines stepping to a Right-Hand Tidal Wave, etc."

So the same idea covers:

| You are in | You step into |
|---|---|
| Facing Dancers | a Right-Hand **Mini-Wave** |
| Facing Couples | a Right-Hand **Ocean Wave** |
| Facing Lines | a Right-Hand **Tidal Wave** |
| an Eight Chain Thru formation | **Parallel Waves** |
| a Right and Left Grand circle | a **Thar** |

## Timing

Applying the rule **does not change the call's timing**:

> "Application of the Facing Couples Rule does not change the timing of the call."

You have the same beats you would have had in a wave. Step in briskly.

## How it differs from the Ocean Wave Rule

They are mirror images, and easy to mix up:

| Rule | You were in | Call expects | Direction of the bridge |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Facing Couples Rule** | Facing Couples / Dancers | a Wave | step **into** the wave |
| [**Ocean Wave Rule**](ocean-wave-rule.md) | a Wave | Facing Couples / Dancers | step **back** into facing |

## At Plus

The Plus program generalises this rule further — to any formation where dancers can step forward to
make a wave. See [Where Mainstream leads](../07-plus/where-mainstream-leads.md).
