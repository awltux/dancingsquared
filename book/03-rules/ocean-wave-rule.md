# Ocean Wave Rule

The mirror of the [Facing Couples Rule](facing-couples-rule.md): some calls are written to start from
**Facing Couples**, and the dancers are already **in a wave**.

> "Some calls that are defined to start from Facing Couples are also proper starting from a
> Right-Hand Ocean Wave. Examples include Right and Left Thru and Square Thru. In these cases, the
> dancers **have already stepped forward** toward the facing dancer and are ready to complete the
> remaining action of the directed call."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

## What you do

You are in a wave, already joined. The call wants you in facing couples. Rather than a step forward,
the rule asks for a step **back**:

> "For the sake of dancer comprehension and teaching purposes, it may be necessary initially to have
> the dancers back up into Facing Couples, then step back into the Wave and complete the call."

You dance the call, and you finish where the call finishes — in the wave, if the call ends in one.

## When it applies

The rule is not a general licence. Two conditions from the manual:

> "In order for the Ocean Wave Rule to apply, the initial dance action of the call must start with a
> **Box the Gnat, Pass Thru, or Right Pull By**."

and:

> "the call must not be defined to have a different dance action from an Ocean Wave."

That second condition has a worked example in the manual worth reading, because it is exactly the
sort of thing a beginner gets caught by:

> "For example, the call Circulate is defined from Eight Chain Thru and from Ocean Waves. It would be
> improper to call Circulate from Ocean Waves and expect dancers to dance it as a Step Thru based on
> the Ocean Wave Rule because they would naturally apply the definition of Circulate from Ocean Waves."

Calls the manual names as examples: **Pass Thru**, **Slide Thru**, **Box the Gnat**, and **Double
Pass Thru** (from a Right-Hand 1/4 Tag).

## It is wider than its name

> "While the rule is called the Ocean Wave Rule, it is generalized to include **Mini-Wave** (Facing
> Dancers) and **Tidal Wave** (Facing Lines), etc."

## The adjustment changes nothing else

> "The adjustment that is part of the Ocean Wave Rule does not change the effect of the call. It
> neither adds nor subtracts parts or changes the use of fractions. It is as if any dancer in a
> mini-wave had first stepped back into facing dancers and then all did the call."

The manual's example is a good one: from a Right-Hand Mini-Wave, **"Dosado" ends in Facing Dancers,
not in a Right-Hand Mini-Wave.** The step back is as if you had started facing.

## Timing

> "Application of the Ocean Wave Rule does not change the timing of the call."

## Mixed formations

Two situations the manual calls out, both of which happen in real squares:

- **Half in a mini-wave, half facing.** When the call is a two-dancer call — *Box the Gnat*,
  *Slide Thru*, *Pass Thru* — the caller should make clear who is to do it: *"Everyone"* or *"Those
  Facing"*.
- **A left-hand wave.** The rule applies there too, but the caller must direct a **left-hand** call,
  for example *"Left Square Thru"*.
