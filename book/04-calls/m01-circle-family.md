# M1 — Circle Family

> **Teaching unit M1** of the CALLERLAB (New) Mainstream Suggested Teaching Order, effective
> 1 September 2026. Prerequisites: [Squared Set](../01-start/the-square.md),
> [Couple handhold](../02-language/hands-and-holds.md).

## Circle Left/Right (1/4, 1/2, 3/4, Full)

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Squared Set](../01-start/the-square.md), [Infacing Circle of 8](../02-language/formations.md#circle), or [Facing Lines](../02-language/formations.md#lines) |
| **Ending formation** | Infacing Circle of 8 — or the Squared Set on "Stop at Home" |
| **Timing** | 1/4: 4 beats · 1/2: 8 beats · 3/4: 12 beats · Full: 16 beats |
| **Unit/part** | M1a of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "Dancers join hands with adjacent dancers to form a circle and move the circle in the indicated
> direction, or to the left if no direction was given."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

Stand in your [Squared Set](../01-start/the-square.md), facing the middle of the square.

1. **Take hands with the dancers either side of you.** It is an ordinary
   [couple handhold](../02-language/hands-and-holds.md) — one hand each side.
2. **Everyone walks in the same direction at the same time.** "Circle Left" means walk forwards to
   your own left. "Circle Right" means the other way. Nobody turns around.
3. **Keep the ring round.** If you drift in or out, the whole circle wobbles and the dancers opposite
   have to hurry.
4. **Stop together when the caller says the fraction.** "Circle Left 1/2" means walk until you are
   half way round the ring from where you started — not half of a personal circle. A
   [fraction](../02-language/fractions.md) is measured around the centre of the ring.
5. **Let go and face in.** You are back in a circle facing the middle, ready for the next call.

You will circle in almost every tip. It is the warm-up, the landing place, and the caller's favourite
way to fill two bars of music.

### Timing

**1/4: 4 beats · 1/2: 8 · 3/4: 12 · Full: 16.** One step per beat, so a full circle is sixteen
walking steps. That is a slow, steady walk — not a march. If the caller is running out of music she
will use a quarter or a half.

### Styling

> "As dancers join hands (Couple handhold) to form a circle, they face slightly left or right as
> directed. All dancers walk forward with joined hands, elbows bent comfortably so that hands are
> above the elbow."

Lean very slightly into the circle so the ring holds its shape, but keep your own weight — nobody
should be hanging on the dancers either side. Elbows stay soft.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| The circle turns into an egg shape | Everyone is pulling outwards | Walk forward, not outwards. Your hands go where your feet take you |
| You meet the dancer opposite face to face | Someone walked the wrong way | "Left" is your left while facing in. Watch the dancer beside you, not the one opposite |
| You stop at the wrong place on "Circle Left 1/2" | Half circle measured as half a turn | A half circle is half of the **ring**, roughly four steps for each pair of dancers opposite each other |
| Hands drop, and the circle breaks | Reaching for a rest | Keep the handhold until the caller's next call; the circle is the grip |

### Where you meet it

Circle Left is the first call most classes teach — [Class 1](../05-course/class-01.md). After that you
will circle at the start and the end of nearly every tip you ever dance, because a circle puts
everyone back where the caller can find them.

### Related calls

- [Circle of 4](#circle-of-4-leftright-14-12-34-full) and [Single Circle](#single-circle-leftright-14-12-34-full) — the small versions, below.
- **[Star Right/Left](m05-star-right-left.md)** — a hands-in-the-middle ring instead of a
  hand-in-hand one.
- **[Wrong Way Promenade](m03-promenade-family.md)** — the same ring, walking the other way as
  couples.

### Also called / formerly

"**Circle**" on its own means Circle Left; callers use it when the music has no room for the extra
word. "**Reverse**" or "**Go the other way**" tells the circle to change direction, usually followed
by "Circle Right". Older programmes taught "Circle Left 1/4" as its own call; the 2026 manual keeps
the fractions inside the one name.

## Circle of 4 Left/Right (1/4, 1/2, 3/4, Full)

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Facing Couples](../02-language/formations.md#facing-couples) |
| **Ending formation** | Facing Couples, turned by the amount you circled |
| **Timing** | 1/2: 4 beats · 3/4: 6 beats · Full: 8 beats |
| **Unit/part** | M1b of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "Same as above. When dancing in a Circle of 4, the dancers end as the same Facing Couples rotated
> by the appropriate amount."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

You are one of two couples facing each other.

1. **Join hands in a ring of four** — the two dancers beside you are your partner and one dancer from
   the facing couple.
2. **Walk, all four together**, in the direction the caller gives. The ring is small, so your steps
   are small too.
3. **Stop at the fraction.** "Circle Four, 1/2 way around" leaves you facing the couple you started
   with, but each of you has swapped the side of the ring you were on.
4. **Drop hands and face the other couple.** You are still [Facing Couples](../02-language/formations.md#facing-couples),
   just rotated.

From [Facing Lines](../02-language/formations.md#lines) the caller must say how many circles to make, so
listen for "Circle Four" or "In Groups of 4".

### Timing

**1/2: 4 beats · 3/4: 6 · Full: 8.** A four-dancer circle is a quarter of the size of the big ring,
so the beats drop with it.

### Styling

> "Same as above. The grapevine step is not recommended in 4-dancer circles."

Same [couple handhold](../02-language/hands-and-holds.md) as the big circle, same soft elbows. Keep
the ring tight — a loose four-dancer circle turns into a square.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| The four of you drift apart into a line | Walking outwards instead of around | The ring has one centre. Walk around it |
| You finish facing your original partner's back | Circled the wrong share | Three-quarters of a small ring is only a few steps. Count the other dancers as they pass |
| The ring becomes a rectangle | Two dancers pulling | Match the dancer opposite you. Small ring, small steps |
| Hands drop early | Reaching for the next call before it comes | Hold the ring until the caller speaks again |

### Where you meet it

[Class 1](../05-course/class-01.md), right after the big circle. Callers use a Circle of 4 whenever
they want two couples turned around without using up the whole square.

### Related calls

- [Circle Left/Right](#circle-leftright-14-12-34-full) — the eight-dancer version, above.
- [Single Circle](#single-circle-leftright-14-12-34-full) — the two-dancer version, below.
- **Star Right/Left** — the same small ring with hands in the middle. See [M5](m05-star-right-left.md).

### Also called / formerly

You will hear "**Circle Four**", "**Circle up 4**", and "**In groups of 4**". They are the same
call.

## Single Circle Left/Right (1/4, 1/2, 3/4, Full)

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Facing Dancers](../02-language/formations.md#facing-dancers) |
| **Ending formation** | Facing Dancers, turned by the amount you circled |
| **Timing** | 1/4: 2 beats · 1/2: 4 beats · 3/4: 5 beats · Full: 7 beats |
| **Unit/part** | M1c of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "The facing dancers join both hands and move around the center point between them in the indicated
> direction, or to the left if no direction was given."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

You are standing facing one other dancer, a step or so apart.

1. **Take both hands** — your right hand in their left, your left hand in their right.
2. **Walk around the point between you.** "Single Circle Left" means walk to your own left; your
   partner walks the same way on their side. The two of you turn like a pair of dancers on a music
   box.
3. **Go exactly as far as the caller says.** There is no default: a bare "Single Circle" is not a
   complete instruction, so you will always hear a [fraction](../02-language/fractions.md) —
   "Single Circle Full Around", "Single Circle Left 1/2".
4. **Drop hands and face each other again**, ready for the next call.

### Timing

**1/4: 2 beats · 1/2: 4 · 3/4: 5 · Full: 7.** It is the shortest walk in the family — a full turn
around a partner is seven steps.

### Styling

> "Dancers join right hand to left hand and left hand to right hand. In the case of a Man facing a
> Woman, they join hands with the Man holding both palms up and the Woman holding both palms down."

Both hands joined is the whole grip, so keep your arms relaxed and let the other dancer set the
size of the turn. At the end, drop hands or take whatever hold the next call needs.

### Restrictions

The manual is clear that the distance has to be said: "The amount to move is specified as a fraction
of a full circle (there is no default)." A caller will not leave the fraction off a Single Circle,
and you should not start walking until you hear it.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| You turn a different amount from your partner | One of you walked further | Both hands are joined — the turn is a single unit. Feel the other dancer's pace |
| You finish facing the wrong way | Turned the wrong way for "Left" | "Left" is your own left while you face your partner, not the caller's left |
| You overrun the fraction | Full circle walked when a half was called | Count the fraction as you start, and keep a small step |
| Hands get crushed together | Pulling inwards | Elbows soft, hands at chest height, no squeezing |

### Where you meet it

[Class 1](../05-course/class-01.md), usually as a change of pace after the bigger circles. It is
also the way callers set up a [Pass Thru](m07-pass-thru.md) or a Star Thru from a facing pair.

### Related calls

- [Circle Left/Right](#circle-leftright-14-12-34-full) and [Circle of 4](#circle-of-4-leftright-14-12-34-full) — the bigger versions, above.
- **Dosado** — the same two dancers, walking around each other without hands. See [M2](m02-dosado.md).
- **Star Right/Left** — hands in the middle rather than both hands joined. See [M5](m05-star-right-left.md).

### Also called / formerly

You will hear "**Circle up 2**" and "**Circle 2**". "Single Circle" is the historical name, and the
2026 manual keeps it because a bigger action has been reduced to two dancers.
