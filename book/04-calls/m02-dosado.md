# M2 — Dosado

> **Teaching unit M2** of the CALLERLAB (New) Mainstream Suggested Teaching Order, effective
> 1 September 2026. Prerequisites: [Facing Dancers](../02-language/formations.md#facing-dancers),
> [Passing Rule](../03-rules/passing-rule.md).

## Dosado

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Facing Dancers](../02-language/formations.md#facing-dancers) |
| **Ending formation** | [Facing Dancers](../02-language/formations.md#facing-dancers) — the pair you started with |
| **Timing** | 8 beats from a Squared Set · 6 beats otherwise |
| **Unit** | M2 of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "Walking a smooth circular path, dancers walk forward, passing right shoulders, slide sideways to
> the right, walk backwards, passing left shoulders, and slide slightly to the left to return to their
> starting position."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

Stand facing one other dancer, close enough to touch hands but with your arms down.

1. **Walk forward, just past your partner's right shoulder.** Eyes up, looking at the dancer you are
   passing. This is the [Passing Rule](../03-rules/passing-rule.md): pass right shoulders unless the
   caller tells you otherwise.
2. **Slide to your right** so that you are now stepping backwards.
3. **Walk backwards, passing your partner's left shoulder.** Your left shoulder passes their left
   shoulder.
4. **Slide a little to your left** and you are exactly where you started, facing the same dancer.

Do not turn your body as you go: you always face the direction you are walking, and the shape of the
path does the work. The whole move is smooth and continuous, like walking around a small circle with
someone rather than two straight lines.

### Timing

**6 beats** for a normal Dosado, **8 beats** when dancers are called out of a
[Squared Set](../01-start/the-square.md) and sent back to it. Six beats is a gentle walk — if you are
rushing, you are probably walking backwards too far.

### Styling

> "Men: arms in natural dance position, right shoulders forward as right shoulders pass, left
> shoulders forward as left shoulders pass. Women: both hands on skirt, moving skirt forward and back
> to avoid opposite dancer, right hand forward as right shoulders pass, left hand forward as left
> shoulders pass."

Arms simply hang in [natural dance position](../02-language/hands-and-holds.md) — no folded arms, no
hands on hips. The skirt work is optional comfort, not a rule for either dancer: keep your hands out
of the way and let your shoulders tell your partner which way you are going.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| You bump shoulders in the middle | Both dancers drifted to the same side | Pass **right** shoulders. Give your partner the room you would want |
| You finish facing a different dancer | You turned as you walked | Walk forwards, then backwards. Let your feet trace the loop |
| You end up a step away from home | Walking backwards too far | The backward steps are the same size as the forward ones |
| You dosado the wrong person | Doing it with the facing dancer out of habit | Listen for "Partner" or "Corner" — the caller may name who to walk around |

### Where you meet it

[Class 1](../05-course/class-01.md), usually in the first lesson, because it needs no hands and no
timing. After that it appears in Mainstream figures constantly as a quick way to change who you are
facing.

### Related calls

- [Left Dosado](#dosado) — the same path with the shoulders swapped: left shoulders first. Say "Left Dosado".
- **[Pass Thru](m07-pass-thru.md)** — the straight-through version with no sideways slide.
- **[Single Circle](m01-circle-family.md)** — the same two dancers, joined by both hands.
- **[Half Sashay](m13-half-sashay-family.md)** — another way to trade places with the dancer you
  face.

### Also called / formerly

You will hear "**Do Sa Do**", "**Do-si-do**", and "**Dos-a-do**". For a Left Dosado, older dancers
may say "**See Saw**" — the 2003 committee dropped that use and asks callers to say "Left Dosado", so
expect to hear the old word only from long-serving callers.
