# M3 — Promenade Family

> **Teaching unit M3** of the CALLERLAB (New) Mainstream Suggested Teaching Order, effective
> 1 September 2026. Prerequisites:
> [Couple handhold](../02-language/hands-and-holds.md),
> [promenade direction](../02-language/directions.md), [Squared Set](../01-start/the-square.md).

## Couples Promenade

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Squared Set](../01-start/the-square.md), Right and Left Grand Circle, or Left-Hand Two-Faced Lines |
| **Ending formation** | Static Square with the Boys at home ("Promenade Home"), or couples facing in |
| **Timing** | 1/4: 4 beats · 1/2: 8 beats · 3/4: 12 beats · Full: 16 beats |
| **Unit/part** | M3a of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "As a unit, each Couple walks in a circular path around the center of the set. If certain Couples
> are named, they promenade around the outside of the set unless directed otherwise."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

You are standing beside your partner, both facing the same way.

1. **Take the promenade hold.** Join your inside hands — the Boy's left to the Girl's left — in front
   of you, and take your other hand on the outside. The recommended grip puts the Boy's left palm up
   and the Girl's left palm down on it, with the Boy's right forearm resting over the Girl's left
   forearm. Any comfortable [couple handhold](../02-language/hands-and-holds.md) works in a class.
2. **Walk forward together, side by side.** Both dancers step at the same time, with the same foot.
   Never let the pair pull apart.
3. **Follow the ring of the square.** Promenade direction is the way circle-dancers walk with their
   left shoulders to the middle, so the whole set travels round as one stream.
4. **"Promenade Home" means stop where the Boy started.** The pair turns as a unit to face the centre
   of the set.
5. **"Promenade, Keep Walking" means do not turn in.** Keep walking until the caller gives the next
   call — usually a Wheel Around or a Bend the Line.

### Timing

**1/4: 4 beats · 1/2: 8 · 3/4: 12 · Full: 16.** "Promenade Home" in a singing call is usually more
than a quarter of the way around the ring, so listen for the caller to add "and keep walking" if she
wants another lap.

### Styling

> "When dancers are not directed to Keep Walking, there are several ways in which they can finish. In
> all cases, handholds should blend smoothly to end with a Couple handhold."

Walk tall and keep the pair compact: on a couples promenade the outside dancer has further to walk
than the inside dancer, so the inside dancer must not hurry ahead. If the set spreads out, the
outside dancers wear themselves out chasing the music.

### Restrictions

Promenade is written for a **Normal Couple** — a Boy on the left, a Girl on the right. The manual
notes that when couples are sashayed or same-gender, the call may need to be workshopped, and
"Promenade Home" can be ambiguous unless the caller says whose home to finish at. From a Right and
Left Grand Circle the call is proper **only** when the Boys are already facing
[promenade direction](../02-language/directions.md).

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| The couple drifts apart | The outside dancer cannot keep up | Walk as one pair, with small steps. Say "step, step" out loud to stay together |
| You arrive early or late at home | Partner pairs started from different spots | The whole set travels as one stream. Keep level with the couple in front |
| You step on your partner's feet | Left feet started on the same beat | One inside leg, one outside leg — your left and your partner's left move together |
| You turn in when the caller wanted "Keep Walking" | Predicting the next call | Wait for the caller. "Keep Walking" means the call is not finished |

### Where you meet it

[Class 1](../05-course/class-01.md). Promenade is used in nearly every tip: it is the caller's
standard way to bring the square home, and it fills eight or sixteen beats of music without any
thinking.

### Related calls

- [Wrong Way Promenade](#wrong-way-promenade) — the same walk, the other way round. See below.
- [Star Promenade](#star-promenade) — a promenade with the four Boys holding a star in the middle. See below.
- **[Wheel Around](m08-wheel-around.md)** — how a promenading couple turns to face in.
- **[Allemande Left](m04-arm-turn-family.md)** — the call that usually lands the square in a
  promenade.

### Also called / formerly

You will hear "**Promenade Home**", "**All Promenade**", "**Promenade, don't stop, don't slow
down**" and "**Heads Promenade Half**". Older programmes called it "**Gents Promenade**" or
"**Ladies Promenade**"; both are still heard and mean the same walk.

## Single File Promenade

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | Infacing Circle of 8, [Squared Set](../01-start/the-square.md), Left-Hand Columns |
| **Ending formation** | Single File Promenade — or the Squared Set on "Single File Promenade Home" |
| **Timing** | Four dancers promenading inside to home: 8 beats |
| **Unit/part** | M3b of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "Designated dancers turn, if necessary, to face promenade direction and move forward around the
> center of the set, in Single File (one behind another). The next call determines when the dancers
> stop and what they do next."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

Only the dancers the caller names do this call. Everyone else stays put.

1. **The named dancers turn to face [promenade direction](../02-language/directions.md)** and walk
   forward in a line, one behind the other — no hands joined.
2. **Keep a steady gap** between you and the dancer in front. Single file means exactly that: one
   lane, no passing.
3. **Walk until the caller says something else.** There is no built-in distance; the next call ends
   the promenade.
4. **"Single File Promenade Home" means** the walk finishes in the Squared Set, each dancer on their
   own home spot.

If you are not named, step back out of the line's path and wait. Called from a circle, the line is
"Circle Right; drop hands; Single File Promenade" — the two calls flow together.

### Timing

**8 beats** for four dancers promenading inside to home. A full eight-dancer single file lap is longer
than a couples promenade, because each dancer walks the whole ring alone.

### Styling

> "In Single File Promenade Man's arms are held in natural dance position; Woman's hands on skirt,
> working with the natural swinging motion."

Arms down and relaxed. No hands on the dancer in front — single file is a line of individuals, not a
train. Keep your spacing and do not cut the corners of the square.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| The line bunches up, then stretches out | Following the dancer in front too closely | Keep a constant arm's length gap and walk at the caller's pace |
| You join hands with the person in front | Instinct from the circle | Single file has no handhold at all |
| You turn in early | Expecting "Home" | Wait for the caller. The line keeps walking until told otherwise |
| Dancers who were not named walk too | Everyone follows the first mover | Only do the call if the caller named you. Otherwise stand still and counter-dance |

### Where you meet it

[Class 1](../05-course/class-01.md), as a change from the couples promenade. It is also the way
callers group dancers for a Star or a Star Promenade.

### Related calls

- [Couples Promenade](#couples-promenade) — the partner version, above.
- [Wrong Way Promenade](#wrong-way-promenade) — the opposite direction. See below.
- [Star Promenade](#star-promenade) — the line turns into a star with couples around it. See below.

### Also called / formerly

You will hear "**Promenade Single File**", "**Boys Promenade inside, go Single File**", and
"**Wrong Way Single File Promenade**" for the line walking in the opposite direction. They are all
the same walk, or its mirror.

## Star Promenade

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | A four-dancer star in the centre of the set, with four dancers around the outside |
| **Ending formation** | Promenade (or Wrong Way Promenade) with the centre dancers still forming a star |
| **Timing** | 1/2: 6 beats · 3/4: 9 beats · Full: 12 beats · Full plus backing out at home: 16 beats |
| **Unit/part** | M3c of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "Directed dancers (for example, "Boys") form (or are already in) a Star and turn it. When they
> encounter the indicated dancer (for example, "Partner") they become a Couple, and Promenade while
> retaining the Center Star."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

The four centre dancers are turning a star. You are one of the four outside.

1. **The centre dancers keep the star turning** with their joined hands in the middle.
2. **As the star comes round, each centre dancer meets a named outside dancer.** "Pick up your
   Partner with an arm around" tells you who to collect.
3. **Put an arm around your new partner's waist** — the centre dancer and the outside dancer become a
   pair, side by side.
4. **Everybody promenades together**, with the star still turning in the middle. The centre dancers
   are the hub; the outside dancers walk the rim.
5. **Carry on until the caller names the next call** — often "Boys Backtrack" or "back out at home".

Because the pairs are arm-in-arm with a star in the middle, everyone stands much closer together than
in an ordinary promenade. Expect a tight, crowded ring, not a wide one.

### Timing

**1/2: 6 beats · 3/4: 9 · Full: 12.** Backing out at home after a full turn takes 16. The star turns
more slowly than a plain promenade because eight dancers are sharing one hub.

### Styling

> "The centers use the same styling as in Right- or Left-Hand Stars (page 56). Couples in the Star
> have their adjacent arms around each other's waists. Any women on the outside use their outside hand
> for skirt work."

The [Palm Star](../02-language/hands-and-holds.md) grip in the middle keeps the hub together: each
centre dancer puts the flat of a hand on the back of the hand in front. Keep the arm around your
partner snug — that contact is what makes the crowded ring feel safe.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| The star wobbles or breaks | Centre hands stacked instead of flat | Palm to back-of-hand, all four hands in one neat pile |
| The pair drifts away from the star | Outside dancer walking too wide | Stay close to the hub. Your outside arm does the steering |
| You collect the wrong partner | Hurrying to the first dancer you meet | Listen for "Partner" or "Corner" before you reach out |
| The set becomes a wide, slow circle | Pairs pulling outwards | The star sets the size. Walk where the hub takes you |

### Where you meet it

[Class 1](../05-course/class-01.md). Callers use it to keep the square moving while the four Boys are
already in the middle — it is also the standard setup for a Wrong Way Promenade.

### Related calls

- **Star Right/Left** — the star on its own, without the promenade. See [M5](m05-star-right-left.md).
- [Couples Promenade](#couples-promenade) — the same walk with no star. See above.
- [Wrong Way Promenade](#wrong-way-promenade) — the direction the star is often sent next. See below.
- **Allemande Left** — the call that often precedes the star. See [M4](m04-arm-turn-family.md).

### Also called / formerly

You will hear "**Star Promenade**" and "**pick up your Partner with an arm around, Star
Promenade**". Some callers add "**Girls Backtrack**" or "**Boys Backtrack**" to unwind it — that is
guidance for the next call, not a different call.

## Wrong Way Promenade

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Squared Set](../01-start/the-square.md), Right-Hand Two-Faced Lines, or a Right and Left Grand Circle |
| **Ending formation** | Static Square with the Boys at home, or couples facing in |
| **Timing** | 1/4: 4 beats · 1/2: 8 beats · 3/4: 12 beats · Full: 16 beats |
| **Unit/part** | M3d of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "Wrong Way Promenade is just like Promenade, except dancers go clockwise (that is, in the opposite
> direction of Promenade)."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

1. **Take the same promenade hold** as an ordinary promenade.
2. **Walk the other way round the square.** The Boy still stands on the Girl's left, so now the
   couple's right shoulders are towards the centre of the set.
3. **Keep the pair together** and stay level with the couples in front.
4. **"Wrong Way Promenade Home" finishes** in the Squared Set, at the Boy's home position.
5. **"Keep walking"** means keep going until the next call.

Wrong Way Promenade is called less often than Promenade, and it is almost always used from
[Normal Couples](../02-language/couples-and-positions.md), which puts the Girls on the inside of the
ring.

### Timing

**1/4: 4 beats · 1/2: 8 · 3/4: 12 · Full: 16.** Exactly the same as Promenade — only the direction
changes, not the speed.

### Styling

> "Same as for Couples Promenade (item a. above)."

The promenade hold, the even steps, and the compact pair all carry over. The only thing that changes
is which way you look as you walk.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| The set splits into two traffic jams | Half the dancers walked the old way | Wait for the word "Wrong Way" and turn as a couple. Everyone travels the same way round |
| You turn in at the wrong home | Reading "Home" as your own spot | Home is the **Boy's** starting position, as in an ordinary promenade |
| The pair collides with the next couple | Cutting across the ring | Walk the outside lane in the direction the caller gave, and keep your spacing |
| You miss the call because "this way" was used | The caller omitted "Wrong Way" | "This way" and "promenade this way" mean follow the direction you are already facing |

### Where you meet it

[Class 1](../05-course/class-01.md). It appears most often straight after an Allemande Left or a
Wrong Way Grand, and it is a common way to fill the last eight beats of a singing call.

### Related calls

- [Couples Promenade](#couples-promenade) — the same walk in the usual direction. See above.
- **Wrong Way Grand** — the same reversal in the no-partners grand. See [M6](m06-right-and-left-grand-family.md).
- [Star Promenade](#star-promenade) — a promenade with a centre star. See above.
- **Allemande Left** — a frequent lead-in. See [M4](m04-arm-turn-family.md).

### Also called / formerly

You will hear "**Promenade Wrong Way**", "**Wrong Way Promenade, keep walking**", and simply
"**this way**" or "**promenade this way**" when the dancers are already facing round the wrong way.
