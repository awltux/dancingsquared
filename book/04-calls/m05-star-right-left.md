# M5 — Star Right/Left

> **Teaching unit M5** of the CALLERLAB (New) Mainstream Suggested Teaching Order, effective
> 1 September 2026. Prerequisites: [Facing Couples](../02-language/formations.md#facing-couples),
> [Palm Star](../02-language/hands-and-holds.md),
> [fractions](../02-language/fractions.md).

## Star Right/Left

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Facing Couples](../02-language/formations.md#facing-couples), [Squared Set](../01-start/the-square.md), or Infacing Circle of 8 |
| **Ending formation** | Star plus an adjustment — each dancer turns to face where the next call needs them |
| **Timing** | 1/2: 4 beats · 3/4: 6 beats · Full: 8 beats |
| **Unit** | M5 of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "The designated dancers form a Star by stepping forward if necessary and placing the appropriate
> hand in the center of the formation. Forming the Star may require a dancer to individually turn in
> place up to 3/8 of a turn. Dancers turn the Star by walking forward in a circle around the center
> of the Star."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

You and three other dancers are going to turn a wheel together, with your hands meeting in the
middle.

1. **Step in and put the right hand in the centre** for a Star Right (also called a Right-Hand Star).
   "Star Left" means left hands in the centre instead.
2. **Stack the hands neatly.** Your palm goes flat against the back of the hand already there, so all
   four hands sit in one pile at the middle of the star. That grip is the
   [Palm Star](../02-language/hands-and-holds.md).
3. **Walk forward around the centre.** The star turns because everybody walks; nobody pushes the pile
   of hands round.
4. **Keep your outside arm relaxed** — down at your side for a Boy, doing skirt work if you wear a
   skirt.
5. **Turn the [fraction](../02-language/fractions.md) the caller asks for.** "Star Right, once
   around" is a full turn. "Star Right 1/2" leaves you on the far side of the star from where you
   started.
6. **Let go and adjust.** A star can finish anywhere, so the very last thing you do is turn to face
   whatever the next call needs.

### Timing

**1/2: 4 beats · 3/4: 6 · Full: 8.** A full star takes eight steps — a comfortable, steady walk.
If the set is enormous the hub gets loose and the star takes longer, so keep the hands stacked tightly
in the middle.

### Styling

> "Palm Star (see "Styling: Hands and Arms", page 68). Men's outside arms in natural dance position,
> women's outside hands work skirt."

The stack of hands stays low, around chest height, with elbows soft. Keep the star round: if one
dancer walks wide, the star becomes an egg and the dancers opposite have to sprint.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| The hands slide off each other | Everyone grabbing the wrists in the middle | Flat palm on the back of the next hand, all four in one pile |
| The star lurches and stops | Somebody is pulling instead of walking | The star is powered by feet, not arms. Walk at the speed of the slowest dancer |
| You finish facing the wrong way | Forgetting the adjustment | The star ends where it ends. Turn on the spot to face the next call |
| The star drifts off centre | Different sized steps | Watch the dancers opposite and match them. Keep the hub over one spot on the floor |

### Where you meet it

[Class 2](../05-course/class-02.md). Stars are everywhere: they start an Allemande Thar-style
sequence, they are the middle of a Star Promenade, and a Right-Hand Star is the most common way to
turn four dancers across the square.

### Related calls

- **[Star Promenade](m03-promenade-family.md)** — a star that grows arms and walks.
- **[Arm Turn](m04-arm-turn-family.md)** — the two-dancer version of the same turning action.
- **[Star Thru](m18-star-thru.md)** — despite the name, a completely different call: a two-dancer
  arch and turn.
- **[Allemande Left](m04-arm-turn-family.md)** — often the call before or after a star.

### Also called / formerly

You will hear "**Right-Hand Star**", "**Left-Hand Star**", "**Star by the Right**", "**Make a
Star**", and "**Star once around**". Some regions dance stars containing Boys with a **Pack-Saddle
Star** — an alternative grip taught locally, not a different call. Two-dancer stars are used in some
classes in place of Arm Turns, but they are rarely called at a dance.
