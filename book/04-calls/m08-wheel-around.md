# M8 — Wheel Around / Reverse Wheel Around

> **Teaching unit M8** of the CALLERLAB (New) Mainstream Suggested Teaching Order, effective
> 1 September 2026. Prerequisites: [Couple](../02-language/formations.md#couple),
> [Couple handhold](../02-language/hands-and-holds.md).

## Wheel Around / Reverse Wheel Around

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Couple](../02-language/couples-and-positions.md) |
| **Ending formation** | [Couple](../02-language/couples-and-positions.md) — facing the opposite way |
| **Timing** | 4 beats |
| **Unit** | M8 of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "For Wheel Around, the Couple, working as a unit, turns half around to the left (180 degrees). The
> left-side dancer backs up while the right-side dancer moves forward. The pivot point is the
> handhold between the two dancers."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

You and your partner are side by side, holding hands, both facing the same way.

1. **Keep the handhold.** It is the pivot — the fixed point the pair turns around. Hold it at about
   waist height, arms relaxed, and do not let go.
2. **The left-side dancer backs up.** If the Boy is on the left, he walks backwards in a small arc.
3. **The right-side dancer walks forward** around the left-side dancer, matching the same arc.
4. **Turn a half turn only** — 180 degrees. You finish side by side again, facing exactly the opposite
   way from where you started.
5. **Take a fresh [couple handhold](../02-language/hands-and-holds.md)** and adjust for the next call.

For **Reverse Wheel Around**, the same thing happens the other way: the pair turns half around to the
right, so the right-side dancer backs up and the left-side dancer walks forward.

### Timing

**4 beats.** A half turn around your own handhold is about four walking steps — the same pace as a
[Pass Thru](m07-pass-thru.md) plus the turn. Do not rush it; a fast Wheel Around throws the pair out
of the set.

### Styling

> "Dancers use a Couple handhold or maintain the handhold from the previous call (for example,
> "Promenade"). They adjust the handhold as they finish Wheel Around (or Reverse Wheel Around) if
> required by the next call."

If you are coming out of a Promenade, keep the promenade hold through the turn rather than stopping to
change hands. The pair moves as one piece — no stepping apart, no swinging wide.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| The pair turns a full circle | "Around" sounds like a full turn | Wheel Around is a **half** turn, 180 degrees |
| You let go halfway | Arms reaching apart | The joined hands are the hinge. Keep them together and the turn keeps its size |
| The pair ends up beside the next couple | Turning in the wrong place | Turn on the spot where you stand. The pair pivots on its own handhold |
| You turn the wrong way | Confusing Wheel Around with Reverse | Wheel Around turns **left**; Reverse Wheel Around turns **right** |

### Where you meet it

[Class 2](../05-course/class-02.md). Wheel Around is the standard way to take a promenading couple,
or a couple walking out of a Pass Thru, and turn them round to face in — which is why it almost always
comes just before a Bend the Line or a Right and Left Thru.

### Related calls

- **[California Twirl](m27-california-twirl.md)** — another way to turn a couple around, with an
  arch.
- **[Courtesy Turn](m09-courtesy-turn.md)** — a two-dancer turn with a different hold and a
  different purpose.
- **[Promenade](m03-promenade-family.md)** — where a Wheel Around most often starts.
- **[Bend the Line](m20-bend-the-line.md)** — a common next call after a Wheel Around.

### Also called / formerly

You will hear "**Wheel Around and make lines**", "**All Wheel Around**", and "**Heads Wheel
Around**". The opposite direction is always "**Reverse Wheel Around**".
