# M11 — Swing

> **Teaching unit M11** of the CALLERLAB (New) Mainstream Suggested Teaching Order, effective
> 1 September 2026. Prerequisites: [Facing Dancers](../02-language/formations.md#facing-dancers),
> [Arm Turn](../04-calls/m04-arm-turn-family.md),
> [Couple handhold](../02-language/hands-and-holds.md).

## Swing

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Facing Dancers](../02-language/formations.md#facing-dancers) — a Boy and a Girl |
| **Ending formation** | [Normal Couple](../02-language/arrangements-and-gender.md), usually facing in |
| **Timing** | at least 4 beats |
| **Unit** | M11 of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "Dancers step forward and slightly to their left, use a ballroom hold, and rotate clockwise as a
> unit for four or more beats of music. As dancers end the swing, the Woman continues turning to her
> right (unrolling along the Man's right arm) until she is facing the same direction as the Man."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

Start facing your partner, a step or so apart.

1. **Step in and slightly to your left.** You are not walking a circle around the room; you and your
   partner share one small pivot point between you.
2. **Take the ballroom hold.** The Boy puts his right hand flat on the Girl's back, on her left
   shoulder blade, and his left hand under her right hand. The Girl rests her left hand lightly on
   the Boy's right upper arm or shoulder and puts her right hand on top of his left.
3. **Walk clockwise together**, turning around that shared pivot point. Keep your steps short and
   even. One step per beat of music is the walking swing; the faster **buzz step** is a small circle
   with the right foot while the left foot pushes, like a scooter.
4. **Stay upright and level.** Lean back only slightly into your partner's arms — that lean is what
   the turn produces, not something you add.
5. **To finish, the Boy stops turning** as soon as the two of you face the right way for the next
   call. The Girl keeps moving, rolling out along his right arm, until she faces the same way he
   does and you are a couple again.

At the end, adjust to a [couple handhold](../02-language/hands-and-holds.md), or to a promenade
handhold if a [Promenade](../04-calls/m03-promenade-family.md) comes next.

The whole thing is a smooth turn, not "walk, stop, turn, stop". Callers often add a **twirl** at the
end when a promenade follows: the Boy raises his left hand over the Girl's head and guides her round.
The twirl is always the Girl's choice.

### Timing

**At least 4 beats**, and the manual gives the timing as *Variable, at least 4*. Most callers let a
swing run 8 to 16 beats because it is the most enjoyable call in the program. What matters is that
both of you take one step per beat and finish together on the phrase the caller asked for.

### Styling

> "The connection generated in the swing is created by centrifugal force. All dancers are responsible
> for holding themselves up. … The remaining arms are held only lightly for balance — too much
> tension here makes the swing less fluid, and getting out of it clumsy."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

The Boy's right hand is **flat** on the Girl's back — not fingertips dug in. Keep little or no
vertical motion: no bouncing, and never lift a lighter partner off the floor.

### Restrictions

The manual gives the starting formation as **Facing Dancers (Man and Woman)**. Two Boys facing, or
two Girls, is not this call. The manual also asks callers not to use choreography that *"relies on a
precise ending position for Swing"* — so the exact spot where you stop is the caller's problem, not
yours.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| You drift halfway across the floor | Both dancers are walking a big circle instead of sharing one pivot point | Keep the pivot between you. Short, even steps in place |
| You feel shoved or pulled | The Boy is driving with his arms | The arms only steady. Each dancer carries their own weight and supplies their own momentum |
| Your feet tangle | The two of you are using different steps | Agree on a walking step or a buzz step. The more experienced dancer adjusts |
| You finish facing the wrong way | The Boy keeps turning past the release | The Boy stops turning when he faces the next call; the Girl rolls out along his right arm |

### Where you meet it

Swing is taught in unit M11, in [Class 3](../05-course/class-03.md) of
[the course](../05-course/course-overview.md). It arrives once you can make an
[Arm Turn](../04-calls/m04-arm-turn-family.md) and then appears in almost every tip you ever dance —
in singing calls it is usually the reward in the middle of the figure.

### Related calls

- **[Allemande Left](m04-arm-turn-family.md)** — the other turning call, done by the forearm.
- **[Courtesy Turn](m09-courtesy-turn.md)** — the other way two dancers end up as a couple.
- **[Promenade](m03-promenade-family.md)** — the twirl out of a Swing leads straight into it.

### Also called / formerly

**"Swing Your Partner"** and **"Swing Your Corner"** are shorthands the manual recognises: they mean
*face that dancer, then Swing*. Older material and many callers say *Gents* and *Ladies* where the
2026 manual says **Boys** and **Girls**; it is the same call.
