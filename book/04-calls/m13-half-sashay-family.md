# M13 — Half Sashay Family

> **Teaching unit M13** of the CALLERLAB (New) Mainstream Suggested Teaching Order, effective
> 1 September 2026. Prerequisites: [Couple](../02-language/formations.md#couple),
> [Normal Couple](../02-language/arrangements-and-gender.md),
> [Couple handhold](../02-language/hands-and-holds.md).

## Half Sashay

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Couple](../02-language/formations.md#couple) |
| **Ending formation** | [Couple](../02-language/formations.md#couple), with the two dancers swapped |
| **Timing** | 4 beats |
| **Unit/part** | M13a of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "Dancers exchange places without changing facing directions. Dancer on the right side steps to the
> left while the dancer on the left steps back, side steps to the right, then steps forward, ending
> as a Couple."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

You and your partner are standing side by side, both facing the same way.

1. **Keep facing the way you are facing.** Half Sashay never turns you round.
2. **If you are on the right, step across to the left**, crossing in front of your partner.
3. **If you are on the left, step back, side-step to the right, then step forward** — three small
   steps that let your partner pass in front of you.
4. **Catch hands again** in a [couple handhold](../02-language/hands-and-holds.md). You have swapped
   sides and are still facing the same direction.

In a Normal Couple the Girl finishes on the left and the Boy on the right; you have become a
[Sashayed Couple](../02-language/arrangements-and-gender.md). **Half Sashay Once and a Half** is the
same action done once and a half, and finishes in a
[Tandem](../02-language/formations.md#tandem) — one dancer directly behind the other.

### Timing

**4 beats.** Two beats for the crossing step, two for the recovery.

### Styling

> "Dancers use a normal Couple handhold, pulling slightly toward each other as they initiate the
> sashay movement. As the dancers complete the call, they catch hands to end in a Couple handhold…"

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

Stay close to your partner. The dancers pass front-to-front, not front-to-back, so the hold can be
kept light throughout.

### Restrictions

The manual is firm about the words. The proper command is **"Half Sashay"**; the shortened
**"Sashay"** *"can be confusing and should not be used."* The terms **"Full Sashay"** and **"Sashay
All the Way Around"** are *"not part of any modern square dance program"* and are *"improper
language."*

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| You end up facing your partner | You turned instead of swapping sides | Face the same way throughout. Only your position changes |
| You both step the same way | Neither dancer checked which side they were on | Right-side dancer steps left; left-side dancer backs up and goes round |
| You are a long stride apart at the end | The left-side dancer stepped too far back | Three short steps, not one big one |
| You end up tangled in a handhold | Trying to keep both hands joined while crossing | Let the handhold go for the crossing step and catch it again at the end |

### Where you meet it

Half Sashay is taught in unit M13, in [Class 4](../05-course/class-04.md) of
[the course](../05-course/course-overview.md). It is small, quick, and used constantly to change
which dancer is on which side of a couple.

### Related calls

- [Rollaway](#rollaway) — the same swap with a full turn by one dancer. See part b below.
- [Ladies In, Men Sashay](#ladies-in-men-sashay) — the sashay idea inside a circle. See part c below.
- **[Right and Left Thru](m10-right-and-left-thru.md)** — a common call that ends with a Half Sashay
  effect.

### Also called / formerly

You will hear the command shortened to **"Sashay"**, and older material calls the finished
arrangement a *Half-Sashayed Couple*. The 2026 manual asks callers not to shorten the command.

## Rollaway

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Couple](../02-language/formations.md#couple), or an infacing circle of eight |
| **Ending formation** | [Couple](../02-language/formations.md#couple) or infacing circle of eight, with the two dancers swapped |
| **Timing** | 4 beats |
| **Unit/part** | M13b of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "From a Couple, the dancer on the right (or the directed dancer) 'rolls' across in front of the
> other dancer, turning a full 360 degrees to end on the other side, as the other dancer steps back
> and then forward, adjusting sideways as necessary, to move smoothly into the vacated position."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

1. **The dancer on the right rolls across the front.** Keep the [couple
   handhold](../02-language/hands-and-holds.md) as you turn one full turn, like a rolling barrel, and
   finish on the other side facing the same way you started.
2. **The other dancer steps back**, lets the roller pass, then steps forward into the space left
   behind. Step sideways if you need to.
3. **Catch hands again** in a couple handhold.

From an **infacing circle of eight**, with Boys and Girls alternating, the Girls roll across the
front of the Boys unless the caller says otherwise — **"4 Ladies Rollaway"**, for example.

### Timing

**4 beats** — the roll and the replacement step happen at the same time.

### Styling

> "Hands held in Normal Couple handhold maintaining good arm tension and connection throughout. From
> a Couple, the Man steps back on slight right diagonal as the Woman folds to face him. Continuing
> the momentum, they change hands."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

The rolling dancer keeps their own feet moving; the partner provides a steady hand, not a shove.

### Restrictions

The manual rules out the old name: **"Rollaway with a Half Sashay"** is *"improper language and
should not be used."* If the preceding call was a [Courtesy Turn](../04-calls/m09-courtesy-turn.md),
the left-hands-joined connection is kept for most of the roll and the handhold changes only near the
end.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| The roll stops half way | The rolling dancer ran out of momentum | Keep walking through the turn; it is a walk with a spin, not a jump |
| The stationary dancer gets bumped | They stepped forward too early | Step back first, wait for the roller to pass, then step forward |
| The handhold breaks | Gripping hard with straight arms | Keep the arms bent and the grip light; change hands as you pass |
| The roller ends facing the wrong way | Only a half turn was made | A Rollaway is a **full 360 degrees** back to the original facing |

### Where you meet it

Rollaway is taught in unit M13, in [Class 4](../05-course/class-04.md) of
[the course](../05-course/course-overview.md). It is the standard way to swap two dancers inside a
couple or a circle without changing the shape around them.

### Related calls

- [Half Sashay](#half-sashay) — the same swap without the turn. See part a above.
- [Ladies In, Men Sashay](#ladies-in-men-sashay) — the circle version of a swap. See part c below.
- **Courtesy Turn** — a common call just before a Rollaway. See [M9](m09-courtesy-turn.md).

### Also called / formerly

You will hear **"Roll the Girl Away"** and **"Roll her Away"**. The old name **"Rollaway with a Half
Sashay"** is improper at Mainstream and should not be used.

## Ladies In, Men Sashay

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | infacing [circle](../02-language/formations.md#circle) of eight, Boys and Girls alternating |
| **Ending formation** | the same circle, with every dancer one place further round |
| **Timing** | 4 beats |
| **Unit/part** | M13c of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "From a Circle Left, the Ladies step forward and pause while the Men continue to move to the left,
> behind, and past one Lady. The Ladies now step back and rejoin hands in a Circle with the Men. From
> a Circle Right, the Men move to the right."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

Everyone is in a circle, holding hands, moving counter-clockwise (a Circle Left).

1. **Girls, step forward into the middle and stop.** You have left a gap in the circle.
2. **Boys, keep circling to the left**, passing behind one Girl and on into the space she left.
3. **Girls, step back out** into the circle where the Boy behind you has just moved on from.
4. **Join hands again.** Everyone has moved one place round the circle.

From a **Circle Right**, the Boys move to the right instead. The manual notes that any dancers can be
asked to go in — **"Men In, Ladies Sashay"**, for example.

### Timing

**4 beats** — two to step in and out, two to travel one place.

### Styling

> "Men's hands in slightly up position ready to rejoin the Women in the Circle. Commonly Women have
> both hands on skirt when moving to the center…"

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

Keep the circle's shape: the dancers still in the circle should keep it round, not bunch up around
the gap.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| The circle collapses | Everyone stopped at once | Only the named dancers stop; the rest keep the circle moving |
| Two dancers end up in one place | A Boy counted the wrong Girl | Pass **one** dancer, then take the space they left |
| The Girls come back too early | Rushing out before the Boy has cleared the gap | Wait, watch the Boy go past, then step back in |
| The circle keeps drifting | Nobody is holding the shape | Keep hands joined where you can and step evenly |

### Where you meet it

Ladies In, Men Sashay is taught in unit M13, in [Class 4](../05-course/class-04.md) of
[the course](../05-course/course-overview.md). Callers use it for variety when the square is already
in a circle, and it always comes in the middle of a run of circle calls.

### Related calls

- [Half Sashay](#half-sashay) — the two-dancer version of a swap. See part a above.
- [Rollaway](#rollaway) — the other circle-friendly swap. See part b above.
- **Circle Left/Right** — the call that normally comes just before and after. See
  [M1](m01-circle-family.md).

### Also called / formerly

**"Ladies Center, Men Sashay"**, **"Men Center, Ladies Sashay"** and **"Men In, Ladies Sashay"** all
describe this call with the roles swapped.
