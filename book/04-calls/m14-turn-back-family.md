# M14 — Turn Back Family

> **Teaching unit M14** of the CALLERLAB (New) Mainstream Suggested Teaching Order, effective
> 1 September 2026. Prerequisites: [Facing Dancers](../02-language/formations.md#facing-dancers),
> [Promenade](../04-calls/m03-promenade-family.md).

## U-Turn Back

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | an individual dancer |
| **Ending formation** | the same dancer, facing the opposite way |
| **Timing** | 2 beats |
| **Unit/part** | M14a of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "The dancer does an individual about-face turn (180 degrees) in place, turning toward Partner
> unless the body flow dictates otherwise. If the dancer does not have a Partner, the dancer turns
> toward the center of the set."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

1. **Stay on your spot.** This is a turn in place, not a step across.
2. **Turn halfway round — 180 degrees** — so you finish facing the opposite direction.
3. **Turn toward your partner** if you have one. That keeps the two of you moving in the same flow.
4. **If you have no partner, turn toward the centre of the set.** If you happen to be facing exactly
   toward or away from the centre, either direction will do.

You can be called to do it on your own — **"U-Turn Back"** — or as a group: **"Boys U-Turn Back"**,
**"Centers U-Turn Back"**. It is also a standard ending to a promenade: **"Promenade, Keep Walking;
Everybody U-Turn Back"**.

### Timing

**2 beats.** It is a quick call — one beat to turn, one beat to settle and rejoin hands.

### Styling

> "Isolated dancer: Arms in natural dance position. Adjacent dancers connected with a handhold:
> Release the handhold, perform the dance action, and reconnect with the appropriate handhold."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

Couples promenading in skater's position — the side-by-side promenade hold — can U-Turn Back without
letting go, by turning **towards each other**.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| You turn 360 degrees | Over-spinning | 180 degrees: you should be exactly reversed, not back where you started |
| You step off your spot | Treating it as a walk-round | Turn in place. Your feet take two small steps, nothing more |
| You turn away from your partner | Turning the "easy" way without looking | Turn toward your partner unless the movement clearly flows the other way |
| The handhold drags you off balance | Trying to keep the hold through the turn | Release the hold, turn, then reconnect |

### Where you meet it

U-Turn Back is taught in unit M14, in [Class 4](../05-course/class-04.md) of
[the course](../05-course/course-overview.md). It is the simplest call in the program and one of the
most useful: it turns a promenade around, and it fixes a square that is facing the wrong way.

### Related calls

- [Backtrack](#backtrack) — the same reversal, done while moving forward. See part b below.
- **[Wheel Around](m08-wheel-around.md)** — a couple reversing direction by turning together.
- **[Promenade](m03-promenade-family.md)** — the call most often followed by an Everybody U-Turn
  Back.

### Also called / formerly

**"Turn Back"** and **"Everybody U-Turn Back"** are common phrasings. Names with *Boys*, *Girls* or
*Centers* in front simply say who does it.

## Backtrack

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Promenade](../04-calls/m03-promenade-family.md), Star Promenade, Single File Promenade, or Wrong Way Promenade |
| **Ending formation** | depends on the starting formation and on who was named |
| **Timing** | 2 beats |
| **Unit/part** | M14b of 50 |

### Animation

**No animation yet.** There is no animation of this call.

### Dance action

> "The designated dancers walk in a small 180 degree arc towards the outside of the set."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

You are moving forward in a promenade or a single file line when the caller names you.

1. **Keep walking forward**, but curve outwards so that you trace a small half-circle — 180 degrees.
2. **You end up walking in the opposite direction** from the one you were going, along the outside of
   the set.
3. **If only some dancers are named**, the others carry on as they were — starring or promenading in
   the centre — and the two groups now travel round the set in opposite directions.

**Everyone designated** leaves the whole set in a single file promenade. That is why the call needs
forward movement both before and after it: you walk *into* the curve and *out* of it.

### Timing

**2 beats** for the turn itself, on top of the walking you were already doing.

### Styling

> "If necessary, release the handhold. Continue with arms in natural dance position."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

If the handhold stops you making the curve, let go of it, make the turn, and pick the hold up again.

### Restrictions

The manual records that **"The Couples version of Backtrack (for example, 'Promenade, Head Couples
Backtrack') is no longer in use."** Backtrack is called to individual dancers or to a named group of
dancers, not to whole couples.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| You turn into the middle of the set | Curving the wrong way | A Backtrack curves towards the **outside** of the set |
| You stop to make the turn | Treating it as a U-Turn Back | Keep walking; the turn is a curved path, not a pivot |
| You bump the dancers you just left | Nobody adjusted the shape | The dancers who did not backtrack stay in the centre; the others take the outside |
| The whole square reverses | Everyone thought they were named | Only the named dancers turn |

### Where you meet it

Backtrack is taught in unit M14, in [Class 4](../05-course/class-04.md) of
[the course](../05-course/course-overview.md), with [Single File
Promenade](../04-calls/m03-promenade-family.md). It gives a caller a way to send part of the square
the other way round without breaking the promenade.

### Related calls

- [U-Turn Back](#u-turn-back) — the in-place version of the same reversal. See part a above.
- **Single File Promenade** — where Backtrack is most often used. See
  [M3](m03-promenade-family.md).
- **Star Promenade** — the other common starting formation. See [M3](m03-promenade-family.md).

### Also called / formerly

You will hear **"Ladies Backtrack"**, **"Boys Backtrack"** and **"Step Out and take a Backtrack"**.
The old couples version is no longer in use at Mainstream.
