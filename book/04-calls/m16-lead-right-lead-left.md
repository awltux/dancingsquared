# M16 — Lead Right / Lead Left

> **Teaching unit M16** of the CALLERLAB (New) Mainstream Suggested Teaching Order, effective
> 1 September 2026. Prerequisites: [Couple](../02-language/formations.md#couple),
> [Couple handhold](../02-language/hands-and-holds.md),

## Lead Right / Lead Left

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Couple](../02-language/formations.md#couple) |
| **Ending formation** | [Couple](../02-language/formations.md#couple), normally facing another couple |
| **Timing** | 4 beats |
| **Unit** | M16 of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "Directed Couple(s), working as a unit, move forward along a 90-degree arc to face the Couple (or
> wall) to their right (for Lead Right) or to their left (for Lead Left)."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

You and your partner are a couple, standing side by side in a [couple
handhold](../02-language/hands-and-holds.md). The caller names you — usually **Heads** or **Sides**.

1. **Turn as a pair, not as two people.** You both rotate the same amount, and the handhold stays
   joined.
2. **Walk forward along a quarter-circle — 90 degrees — to your right** for a Lead Right. For a Lead
   Left, the quarter-circle goes to the left.
3. **Stop when you are facing the next couple** (or the wall, if there is no couple there) and settle
   into a couple handhold again.

Picture a clock face: if you were facing twelve o'clock at the start, a Lead Right finishes you
facing three o'clock, and a Lead Left finishes you facing nine o'clock.

Most often the caller says **"Heads Lead Right"** from a squared set. The Heads lead out and end
facing the Sides on the right; that arrangement is called an **Eight Chain Thru formation**, and it is
the usual doorway into the [Square Thru](../04-calls/m21-square-thru.md) family.

### Timing

**4 beats** — two beats to make the turn, two to arrive and settle.

### Styling

> "A Couple handhold is maintained throughout the call. Outside hands in normal dance position."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

Keep the joined hands low and relaxed; this is a walk round a corner, not a swing.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| The couple drifts apart | Each dancer turned a different amount | Move as one unit; the joined hands are the pivot |
| You end facing back into the square | You turned the wrong way, or turned too far | A Lead Right is a quarter turn to the **right**; check the ending facing before you stop |
| The handhold breaks | Arms used to push or pull | Keep the hold light and let your feet do the walking |
| You stop short of the facing couple | Walking the arc instead of the full quarter | Finish your arc, then check: there should be a couple straight in front of you |

### Where you meet it

Lead Right and Lead Left are taught in unit M16, in [Class 4](../05-course/class-04.md) of
[the course](../05-course/course-overview.md). This is one of the first calls that moves a couple
away from the squared set, and it is the usual way a caller gets the square into an Eight Chain Thru
formation.

### Related calls

- **[Veer Left / Veer Right](m17-veer-left-veer-right.md)** — the same quarter-turn idea, but the
  couple keeps moving forward.
- **[Right and Left Thru](m10-right-and-left-thru.md)** — the usual first call after a Lead Right.
- **[Star Thru](m18-star-thru.md)** — another way to turn a facing pair into a couple.

### Also called / formerly

You will hear **"Lead Out to the Right"** and **"Couple #1 Lead Out to the Right"**. Some callers say
*"Lead Out"* with no direction and let the flow of the previous call suggest it; when the direction
matters, say it.
