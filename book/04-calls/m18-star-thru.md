# M18 — Star Thru

> **Teaching unit M18** of the CALLERLAB (New) Mainstream Suggested Teaching Order, effective
> 1 September 2026. Prerequisites: [Facing Dancers](../02-language/formations.md#facing-dancers),
> [Couple handhold](../02-language/hands-and-holds.md).

## Star Thru

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Facing Dancers](../02-language/formations.md#facing-dancers) — a Boy facing a Girl |
| **Ending formation** | [Couple](../02-language/formations.md#couple) |
| **Timing** | 4 beats |
| **Unit** | M18 of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "Man places his right hand against Woman's left hand, palm to palm with fingers up, to make an
> arch. As the dancers move forward the Woman does a one quarter (90 degrees) left face turn under
> the arch, while the Man does a one quarter (90 degrees) turn to the right moving past the Woman."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

Stand facing your partner, a step or so apart.

1. **Make the arch.** Reach forward and join **your right hand to their left hand**, palm to palm,
   fingers pointing up. It is a handshake held high — around eye level — not a grab.
2. **Both walk forward.**
3. **The Girl turns left a quarter turn, under the arch.** You are turning into the space beside
   your partner, not walking through them.
4. **The Boy turns right a quarter turn**, passing behind.

You finish **side by side, both facing the same way** — a couple. Drop the arch and take an ordinary
[couple handhold](../02-language/hands-and-holds.md).

The whole thing is one smooth movement, not four separate steps. From a squared set you can do it in
about two seconds.

### Timing

**4 beats** — two bars of music at a walking pace. In a singing call that is half of one 8-beat
phrase, which is why callers can slip a Star Thru into a phrase without disturbing the
dancers' feet.

### Styling

> "Hands are joined in raised position at approximately eye level, palm to palm, with fingers pointed
> up to form an arch. The arch will be offset to the Man's right and Woman's left. … As the call is
> completed, the hand grip should be readjusted to couple handhold."

The handhold is **loose**. The Boy steadies; the Girl supplies her own momentum. Do not push your
partner through the turn.

### Restrictions

The 2026 manual gives the starting formation as **Facing Dancers (Man facing Woman)**. Two Boys
facing, or two Girls, is **not** Star Thru — there is no Boy/Girl arch to make. When the square is
arranged so that you are facing someone of the same role, the caller will use a different call.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| The arch collapses and you end up in a tangle | Both dancers are pulling | The arch is a hinge, not a lever. Keep it light and high |
| You finish facing your partner again | You turned back instead of through | The Girl turns **left**, the Boy turns **right** — you are swapping sides, not bouncing |
| You end up a step too far apart | Walking forward too far before turning | Turn *as* you walk; the turn is the last half of the forward step |
| Hands come down too early | Reaching for a couple handhold mid-call | Keep the arch until you are facing the same way |

### Where you meet it

Star Thru arrives early — [Class 5](../05-course/class-05.md) — and then appears everywhere for the
rest of your dancing. It is the standard way to turn a facing pair into a couple, and it is one of
the few calls you will use in almost every tip you ever dance.

### Related calls

- **[Slide Thru](m28-slide-thru.md)** — the same shape with a different hand action.
- **[Box the Gnat](m30-box-the-gnat.md)** — the same turn with a *pull by* instead of an arch.
- **[California Twirl](m27-california-twirl.md)** — the arch-and-turn idea used to turn a couple
  around.

### Also called / formerly

Star Thru has always been called Star Thru. You will hear **"Star Through"** in some regions; it is
the same call.
