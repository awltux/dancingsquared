# M19 — Circulate Family

> **Teaching unit M19** of the CALLERLAB (New) Mainstream Suggested Teaching Order, effective
> 1 September 2026. Prerequisites: [Waves](../02-language/formations.md#waves),
> [Lines](../02-language/formations.md#lines), [Columns](../02-language/formations.md#columns),
> [Box](../02-language/formations.md#box),
> [Passing Rule](../03-rules/passing-rule.md).

## Couples Circulate

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Two-Faced Lines](../02-language/formations.md#lines) |
| **Ending formation** | [Two-Faced Lines](../02-language/formations.md#lines) |
| **Timing** | 4 beats |
| **Unit/part** | M19a of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "Each Couple works as a unit to move forward to the next position, following the General Box
> circulate path."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

You are in a [Two-Faced Line](../02-language/formations.md#lines): you and your partner side by side, your
couple back to back with the other couple, all four of you facing outwards.

1. **Stay with your partner.** The couple is the unit here, not the individual.
2. **Walk forward to the next couple's position**, following the rectangle the four of you make. The
   couple who are facing out at one end of the box travel round and end where the other couple
   started, and the other couple do the same.
3. **Finish facing the same way couples face in a two-faced line** — each couple facing outwards
   again.

The couple who has the shorter distance to travel takes shorter steps so that both couples arrive
together.

### Timing

**4 beats.** A full circulate is a walk of one position.

### Styling

> "All dancers maintain a Couple handhold for Couples Circulate. Couples traveling the shorter
> distance should adjust with shorter steps to coincide with those traveling the longer distance.
> Those traveling the longer distance should not rush."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

Keep the couple handhold and stay level with your partner. The two couples in the box must arrive at
the same moment.

### Restrictions

**No call in the Circulate Family can be used with the [Ocean Wave Rule](../03-rules/ocean-wave-rule.md)
or the [Facing Couples Rule](../03-rules/facing-couples-rule.md).** A circulate needs the formation it
names; dancers cannot step into it from a nearby formation the way other calls allow.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| The couples collide | One couple hurried the longer path | The shorter-path couple takes shorter steps; nobody rushes |
| You split from your partner | Circulating as an individual instead of a couple | Couples Circulate moves the **couple**; keep the handhold |
| You finish facing the wrong way | Half a circulate, or one too many | One position forward, facing out, is the whole call |
| The box loses its shape | Couples took different size paths | Keep the rectangle; walk the path, do not cut across |

### Where you meet it

Couples Circulate is taught in unit M19, in [Class 5](../05-course/class-05.md) of
[the course](../05-course/course-overview.md). It is the first circulate most dancers meet, and it
appears in almost every tip that uses two-faced lines.

### Related calls

- [(Named Dancers) Circulate](#named-dancers-circulate) — the same path for individual dancers. See part b below.
- [All Eight Circulate](#all-eight-circulate) — everybody circulates. See part c below.
- **[Bend the Line](m20-bend-the-line.md)** — the other standard call from a two-faced line.

### Also called / formerly

Couples Circulate has always been called Couples Circulate. Many callers shorten it to **"Couples
Circ"** in a singing call; it is the same call.

## (Named Dancers) Circulate

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | general [Lines](../02-language/formations.md#lines) (Ocean Waves, Two-Faced Lines) or general [Columns](../02-language/formations.md#columns) |
| **Ending formation** | the same formation you started in |
| **Timing** | 4 beats; 2 if the circulate has the effect of a Pass Thru |
| **Unit/part** | M19b of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "Designated dancers move forward to the next dancer position, following their General Lines or
> General Columns circulate path. A dancer looking out of the formation walks in a 180-degree arc and
> finishes in the next position on the other side, looking in. Others walk forward one dancer
> position."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

The caller names a group — **Centers**, **Ends**, **Boys**, **Girls**, **Everyone** — and only they
move.

1. **Find your circulate path.** In a line, the path runs along the line; in a column, it runs along
   the column. The manual's diagrams call these the *General Lines* and *General Column* paths.
2. **Walk forward one dancer position.**
3. **If you are the dancer on the end looking out of the formation**, you do not just walk: you travel
   round a half circle — 180 degrees — and finish in the next position on the far side, facing back
   in.
4. **If you are not named, stand still.** You are the fixed point everyone else moves around.

Two dancers travelling in opposite directions along the same path pass **right shoulders**, exactly
as in the [Passing Rule](../03-rules/passing-rule.md).

**"Circulate"** on its own means *Everyone Circulate*, unless the previous call made it obvious that
only some dancers are still active.

### Timing

**4 beats** — or **2 beats** when the circulate has the effect of a
[Pass Thru](../04-calls/m07-pass-thru.md), which happens when dancers travel past each other rather
than round a path.

### Styling

> "Arms should be held in natural dance position and ready to assume appropriate position for the next
> call."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### Restrictions

The manual states it is **improper** for a named circulate to make a moving dancer end on the same
spot as a dancer who is standing still — *"from Columns, 'Ends Circulate'"*, for example. From
Columns, *"Centers Circulate"* is proper only when the caller makes clear that the centres stay in
the centre.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| The wrong dancers move | "Centers" and "Ends" confused | In a wave or line, centers are the middle two of the four; ends are the outside two |
| The end dancer walks straight off the line | Ignoring the 180-degree arc | The dancer looking out turns half round as they travel |
| Two dancers crash | Nobody applied the shoulder rule | Opposite directions on one path pass right shoulders |
| Movers end up on top of standers | The named group cannot legally reach that spot | Listen for the caller to name a different group, or a Box Circulate |

### Where you meet it

Named Circulate is taught in unit M19, in [Class 5](../05-course/class-05.md) of
[the course](../05-course/course-overview.md), right after Couples Circulate. Callers use it to move
half the square at a time and to set up the next formation.

### Related calls

- [Couples Circulate](#couples-circulate) — the couple version. See part a above.
- [All Eight Circulate](#all-eight-circulate) — everybody moves. See part c below.
- **Split Circulate** — halve the formation and circulate within each half. See part e below.

### Also called / formerly

You will hear **"Centers Circulate"**, **"Ends Circulate"**, **"Girls Circulate"**, **"Boys
Circulate"** and **"Everyone Circulate"**. The manual also lets a caller name a number — **"Center 4
Circulate"**, **"Outside 6 Circulate"**.

## All Eight Circulate

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | general [Lines](../02-language/formations.md#lines) or general [Columns](../02-language/formations.md#columns) |
| **Ending formation** | the same formation you started in |
| **Timing** | 4 beats; 2 if the circulate has the effect of a Pass Thru |
| **Unit/part** | M19c of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "All dancers Circulate."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

1. **Everybody moves at once.** There is nobody to stand still for.
2. **Follow your own circulate path** — along the line if you are in a line, along the column if you
   are in a column.
3. **The dancer looking out of the formation turns a half circle** and comes back in on the far side.
4. **Finish in the same formation you started in**, facing the same way, with a different dancer in
   each position.

The manual warns against a common piece of bad teaching: it is **improper** to say that All Eight
Circulate always means *"ends stay ends and centers stay centers."* That is true from Ocean Waves and
Two-Faced Lines, but it is **not** true from Columns. From Columns, All Eight Circulate is the same
thing as [Single File Circulate](#single-file-column-circulate).

### Timing

**4 beats**, or **2** when the movement has the effect of a
[Pass Thru](../04-calls/m07-pass-thru.md).

### Styling

> "Same as for (Named Dancers) Circulate (item b. above)."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

In other words: arms in natural dance position, hands ready for the next call.

### Restrictions

As with every circulate, the [Ocean Wave Rule](../03-rules/ocean-wave-rule.md) and the
[Facing Couples Rule](../03-rules/facing-couples-rule.md) do not apply. The manual also notes that
from Columns, All Eight Circulate is the same as Single File Circulate — callers should use the more
descriptive name.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| You keep your "role" out of habit | Believing ends stay ends in every formation | In Columns, everybody simply moves one place along the column |
| The wave loses its handholds | Letting go because everyone is moving | Keep arms in dance position and rejoin hands in the new wave |
| You and a neighbour cross awkwardly | Ignoring the shoulder rule | Opposite directions on one path pass right shoulders |
| The whole square drifts | Nobody kept the shape | Circulate inside the formation; do not travel beyond it |

### Where you meet it

All Eight Circulate is taught in unit M19, in [Class 5](../05-course/class-05.md) of
[the course](../05-course/course-overview.md). It is the simplest way to keep a formation's shape
while changing who stands where.

### Related calls

- [Couples Circulate](#couples-circulate) — the version for two-faced lines, and the more usual way to call this from
  there. See part a above.
- [Single File (Column) Circulate](#single-file-column-circulate) — identical to this call from Columns. See part d below.
- [(Named Dancers) Circulate](#named-dancers-circulate) — the version where only some dancers move. See part b above.

### Also called / formerly

The manual writes the call as **All 8 Circulate**; most callers say **"All Eight Circulate"**. Both
are in use.

## Single File (Column) Circulate

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Columns](../02-language/formations.md#columns) only |
| **Ending formation** | [Columns](../02-language/formations.md#columns) |
| **Timing** | 4 beats; 2 if the circulate has the effect of a Pass Thru |
| **Unit/part** | M19d of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "All dancers Circulate."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

You are in a [Column](../02-language/formations.md#columns): a line of dancers, one behind the other, all
facing along the column.

1. **Walk forward one dancer position**, along the column.
2. **The dancer at the front** — the one looking out of the formation — travels round a half circle
   and finishes at the back of the same column, facing back in the way the others are.
3. **Keep your facing.** A column circulate does not turn anybody round except the dancer at the
   front, who turns through 180 degrees to rejoin the column.

You will hear this called **"Circulate"** when the square is already in columns, because there is
only one circulate it can mean; **"Column Circulate"** and **"Single File Circulate"** say it
explicitly.

### Timing

**4 beats** — or **2** when the movement has the effect of a
[Pass Thru](../04-calls/m07-pass-thru.md).

### Styling

> "Same as for (Named Dancers) Circulate (item b. above)."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### Restrictions

The manual gives the starting formation as **Columns only**. As with every circulate, the
[Ocean Wave Rule](../03-rules/ocean-wave-rule.md) and the
[Facing Couples Rule](../03-rules/facing-couples-rule.md) do not apply.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| The front dancer stops dead | Forgetting the 180-degree arc | The front dancer turns half round and joins the back of the same column |
| Dancers swap columns | Walking into the neighbouring column | Stay in your own column |
| The column concertinas | Different step sizes | One position, even steps, everybody together |
| The column turns into a line | Facing lost during the walk | Keep facing along the column; only the front dancer turns |

### Where you meet it

Single File Circulate is taught in unit M19, in [Class 5](../05-course/class-05.md) of
[the course](../05-course/course-overview.md). Columns are where [Wheel and
Deal](../04-calls/m26-wheel-and-deal.md) and the tag calls begin, so this circulate shows up
constantly in later figures.

### Related calls

- [All Eight Circulate](#all-eight-circulate) — the same thing from Columns. See part c above.
- [Couples Circulate](#couples-circulate) — the two-faced-line version. See part a above.
- **Wheel and Deal** — the standard way out of columns. See [M26](m26-wheel-and-deal.md).

### Also called / formerly

The manual's index writes it as **Single File Circulate**; the call map and many callers say
**Column Circulate**. In columns, plain **"Circulate"** means this call.

## Box/Split Circulate

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Box](../02-language/formations.md#box) for Box Circulate; general Lines or Columns for Split Circulate |
| **Ending formation** | the same formation you started in |
| **Timing** | 4 beats; 2 if the circulate has the effect of a Pass Thru |
| **Unit/part** | M19e of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> **Box Circulate:** "Each dancer moves forward to the next position, following the General Box
> circulate path."
>
> **Split Circulate:** "Divide ('split') the formation in half. Dancers in each half move forward to
> the next position, following the General Box circulate path."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

**Box Circulate.** Four of you are in a small two-by-two box. Each dancer walks forward to the next
position in that box, following the rectangle it makes. The two dancers who are facing out travel
round and come back in on the other side; the two facing in walk straight forward. The box keeps its
shape and you end in a different position inside it.

**Split Circulate.** The caller divides a larger formation — a line of eight, or a column of eight —
into two halves. Each half is a box, and each half circulates as above. The two halves do the same
movement side by side and do not cross into one another.

The manual allows Box Circulate only after a group of four has been named — **"Heads Touch a Quarter;
Heads Box Circulate"**, **"Centers Box Circulate"**. Once the four dancers are identified, Box
Circulate and Circulate are the same thing, and many callers just say **"Circulate"**.

### Timing

**4 beats**, or **2** when the movement has the effect of a
[Pass Thru](../04-calls/m07-pass-thru.md).

### Styling

> "Same as for (Named Dancers) Circulate (item b. above)."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

Arms in natural dance position, hands ready for the next call.

### Restrictions

The manual states that **Box Circulate is proper only after specifying a group of four dancers** (or,
rarely, groups of four). "Split Circulate" is the preferred name when the whole formation is being
divided. As with every circulate, the [Ocean Wave Rule](../03-rules/ocean-wave-rule.md) and the
[Facing Couples Rule](../03-rules/facing-couples-rule.md) do not apply.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| You circulate round the whole square | Confusing Box Circulate with All Eight | Box Circulate moves you inside your own group of four, nothing more |
| The two halves of a split merge | Nobody kept the halves apart | Each half is its own box; do not cross the middle |
| You end facing the wrong way | Missing the 180-degree arc for the outward-facing dancer | The dancer looking out turns a half circle and comes back in |
| Nobody knows who is in the box | The group was not identified | Wait for the caller to name four dancers, or to say "Split Circulate" |

### Where you meet it

Box and Split Circulate are taught in unit M19, in [Class 5](../05-course/class-05.md) of
[the course](../05-course/course-overview.md). They are the calls that make small groups of four
work independently, and they are the reason a caller can move one corner of the square without
touching the rest.

### Related calls

- [(Named Dancers) Circulate](#named-dancers-circulate) — the general named-group version. See part b above.
- [All Eight Circulate](#all-eight-circulate) — everybody, all at once. See part c above.
- **Split Two** — the other way to divide a formation in half. See [M22](m22-split-two.md).

### Also called / formerly

The manual handles these as two separate cases under one heading and calls them **Box Circulate** and
**Split Circulate**. You will also hear **"Centers Box Circulate"** and **"Split Circulate"**.
