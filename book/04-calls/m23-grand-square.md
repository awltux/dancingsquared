# M23 — Grand Square

> **Teaching unit M23** of the CALLERLAB (New) Mainstream Suggested Teaching Order, effective
> 1 September 2026. Prerequisites: [Static Square](../01-start/the-square.md),
> [Heads and Sides](../02-language/naming-the-dancers.md),
> [Couple handhold](../02-language/hands-and-holds.md).

## Grand Square

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Static Square](../01-start/the-square.md), after the caller names who faces |
| **Ending formation** | [Static Square](../01-start/the-square.md) |
| **Timing** | 32 beats — eight phrases of four |
| **Unit** | M23 of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "On "Step (1), Step (2), Step (3), Step (4)" dancers who are facing nose-to-nose take 4 steps
> backwards away from each other. Dancers who are facing across the square take 4 steps forward until
> they are nose-to nose."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

The manual adds that on the fourth step "dancers turn in place 90 degrees during the step to face
another dancer nose-to-nose or across the square", and that this same group of four steps then
repeats.

### What you do

Grand Square is the longest call in the programme and the one beginners find hardest — not because
any single step is difficult, but because it runs for 32 beats without the caller saying anything
more. Do it by counting.

1. **Listen for who faces.** The caller says "Sides Face, Grand Square" or "Heads Face, Grand
   Square". If you are named, **turn where you stand to face your partner**. If you are not named,
   keep facing across the square, towards the dancer opposite you.
2. **Dance eight phrases of four counts.** Each phrase is the same: **step (1), step (2), step (3),
   step and turn a quarter turn in place (4)**.
3. **Know which way you travel.** If you are nose to nose with someone, those four steps go
   **backwards**, away from each other. If you are facing across the square, they go **forwards**
   until you are nose to nose.
4. **On count 4, turn a quarter turn in place.** You are now facing the next dancer, either nose to
   nose or across the square. Who is nose to nose swaps over each phrase.
5. **On count 16, do not turn.** You step and pause. That is exactly halfway, and the caller may say
   "Reverse". From here you **retrace the whole thing backwards**.
6. **On count 32 you are home.** The dancers who faced at the start turn back in to make the square
   again; the others take their last step and stand still.

The manual is emphatic about one thing: "these are square dancing steps and NOT just walking". Three
steps and a turn, in time, for eight phrases. If you walk it, you will be out of position long before
the end. See [Timing and music](../01-start/timing-and-music.md).

### Timing

**32 beats**, and it must not be rushed: one Step and one Step-Turn In per beat of music, so that
everybody arrives home on the same beat. The manual also asks callers to start Grand Square on beat 1
of an 8-beat phrase, preferably beat 1 of a 32-beat phrase, so the eight four-count phrases line up
with the music.

### Styling

> "Men's arms in natural dance position; women may work skirts with natural swinging action. Adjacent
> dancers who walk forward or backward together should use a Couple handhold."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

The dancers moving together down a side of the square hold an ordinary
[couple handhold](../02-language/hands-and-holds.md). Grand Square attracts embellishments — twirls
and extra turns — and the manual notes you may meet "a wide variety". None of them are required, and
none of them are worth missing a beat for.

### Restrictions

The manual is explicit about how Grand Square may be started:

> "From a Static Square, the caller must designate who faces for Grand Square. It is improper to call
> "Grand Square" and expect "Sides Face, Grand Square.""

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

So the call on its own is incomplete: somebody has to be told to face. The manual adds a second
condition that dancers can check for themselves — "At all times during Grand Square, every dancer is
facing another dancer either nose-to-nose or across the square" — and notes that the relaxed versions
("Girls turn and face your partner, Everyone Grand Square") may need workshopping. See
[Mainstream restrictions](../03-rules/mainstream-restrictions.md).

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| You are half a beat behind by count 16 | Walking the sides instead of dancing them | Step on every count. Step, step, step, and turn on the fourth |
| You turn on count 16 and end up facing the wrong way | The pause is easy to miss | Count 16 is a Step and a **pause**, not a turn. That is how the reverse works |
| You walk forwards when you should back up | Losing track of whether you are nose to nose or across | Look at the dancer in front of you. Nose to nose means back up; across the square means come forward |
| You and your partner drift apart | Dropping the handhold and moving at different speeds | Take a couple handhold whenever you and the adjacent dancer move together |

### Where you meet it

Grand Square is taught in [Class 6](../05-course/class-06.md). At first the caller will talk you
through it, and in a class you may well dance it in small pieces; by the time you have it, it is one
of the most satisfying calls in the programme, and it appears in singing calls regularly. Callers may
also shorten it — "Grand Square, 6 Steps" — so listen for a number.

### Also called / formerly

Grand Square has always been called Grand Square. What changes is the words in front of it: **"Sides
Face, Grand Square"**, **"Heads Face, Grand Square"**, or a named couple. Halfway through you may hear
**"Reverse"** — that is the caller reminding you of the count-16 pause, not a new call. Older material
and many callers say "Men and Women" or "Gents and Ladies" where this book says Boys and Girls.
