# M24 — Double Pass Thru

> **Teaching unit M24** of the CALLERLAB (New) Mainstream Suggested Teaching Order, effective
> 1 September 2026. Prerequisites: [Facing Tandems](../02-language/formations.md#tandem),
> [Passing Rule](../03-rules/passing-rule.md), [Ocean Wave Rule](../03-rules/ocean-wave-rule.md).

## Double Pass Thru

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Facing Tandems](../02-language/formations.md#tandem) — one dancer directly behind another |
| **Ending formation** | [Back-to-Back Tandems](../02-language/formations.md#tandem) |
| **Timing** | 4 beats |
| **Unit** | M24 of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "Dancers move forward until all dancers have passed two other dancers. Dancers pass right
> shoulders."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

You are standing behind your partner, both facing the same way. Ahead of you is another dancer, also
standing behind their partner. That is a [facing tandem](../02-language/formations.md#tandem) — a column of
two facing a column of two.

1. **Walk straight forward**, keeping your partner directly in front of you.
2. **Pass the front dancer of the other tandem** right shoulder to right shoulder.
3. **Keep going**, and pass their back dancer right shoulder to right shoulder as well.
4. **Stop when you have passed both of them**, and then step apart far enough that the other tandem
   can also end up standing in a line. You finish back to back with the tandem you passed.

The name says exactly what happens: *double* pass through — you pass two dancers, not one. Pass Thru
passes one. The whole call is four beats, so it is a brisk walk, not a stroll. See
[Passing Rule](../03-rules/passing-rule.md) for why it is the right shoulder you use.

### Timing

**4 beats** — two bars of music. Because it is so short, Double Pass Thru is nearly always the first
half of a longer figure: "Double Pass Thru, Leaders Trade", "Double Pass Thru, Centers In", or
"Double Pass Thru; First Couple Go Left, Next Couple Go Right". Be ready to keep moving when it ends.

### Styling

> "Same as for Pass Thru (page 44)."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

In practice that means arms in natural dance position, no handhold needed along the way, and enough
space between the two tandems that nobody collides. Teach yourself the same habits as for
[Pass Thru](m07-pass-thru.md): walk on a straight line, look at the dancer you are about to pass, and
do not drift sideways.

### Restrictions

> "Except for applications of the Ocean Wave Rule, dancers must start facing each other, rather than
> offset."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

That means the two tandems must line up squarely, head on. The manual's own example is that "Heads
Star Thru, Boys Double Pass Thru" is **improper**, because that leaves the Boys offset rather than
facing. The legitimate version is "Heads Star Thru, Boys on a Diagonal, Double Pass Thru", which is a
proper extension and ends in Left-Hand Columns. The manual also notes that the
[Ocean Wave Rule](../03-rules/ocean-wave-rule.md) applies, so it can begin from a Right-Hand 1/4 Tag
(or a Right-Hand Single 1/4 Tag). See
[Mainstream restrictions](../03-rules/mainstream-restrictions.md).

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| You drift sideways and the tandems cross badly | Aiming at the gap instead of walking straight | Pick a line and walk it. The other tandem is doing the same |
| You stop after passing one dancer | Thinking it is Pass Thru | Count them: Double means two dancers passed |
| You finish facing your partner | Turning round during the pass | There is no turn in Double Pass Thru. You finish back to back |
| The square ends up in a muddle | The two tandems started offset | Start squarely facing each other, or the caller must make it proper first |

### Where you meet it

Double Pass Thru is taught in [Class 6](../05-course/class-06.md), with
[Wheel and Deal](m26-wheel-and-deal.md) and [California Twirl](m27-california-twirl.md). It is a
cornerstone of modern choreography: the formation it ends in, back-to-back tandems, is where
"Leaders Trade", "Centers In" and "First Couple Go Left" all begin.

### Related calls

- **[Pass Thru](m07-pass-thru.md)** — the same idea with one dancer passed instead of two.
- **[Dive Thru](m29-dive-thru.md)** — a couple ducks under an arch and ends in the same back-to-back
  tandem shape.
- **[Wheel and Deal](m26-wheel-and-deal.md)** — the usual next call from the line that follows.
- **[Centers In](m44-centers-in.md)** — one of the standard ways out of the finished formation.

### Also called / formerly

Double Pass Thru has always been called Double Pass Thru. The manual records the extended relatives
you will hear older dancers mention — **Triple Pass Thru** from facing tandems of three, and
**Quadruple Pass Thru** from facing tandems of four — and adds that dancers are not expected to know
those without being told first.
