# M25 — First Couple Go Left/Right, Next Couple Go Right/Left

> **Teaching unit M25** of the CALLERLAB (New) Mainstream Suggested Teaching Order, effective
> 1 September 2026. Prerequisites: [Completed Double Pass Thru](../02-language/formations.md#double-pass-thru),
> [Couple handhold](../02-language/hands-and-holds.md), [Wheel Around](m08-wheel-around.md).

## First Couple Go Left/Right, Next Couple Go Right/Left

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | Completed [Double Pass Thru](../02-language/formations.md#double-pass-thru) |
| **Ending formation** | [Facing Lines](../02-language/formations.md#lines) — or a [Promenade](../02-language/formations.md#promenade) if both couples are sent the same way |
| **Timing** | 6 beats |
| **Unit** | M25 of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "Dancers, with each Couple working as a unit, walk forward in an arc in the indicated direction. The
> Center Couple walks forward before taking the second direction given. If the Couples are asked to go
> in opposite directions (for example, "First Couple Go Left, Next Couple Go Right"), they should move
> in a tight circle in their specified direction for half a circle."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

This call is one sentence of walking, and the only hard part is knowing which couple you are.

1. **Look at where you are.** You finished a [Double Pass Thru](m24-double-pass-thru.md), so you are
   standing side by side with your partner, facing out of the square, with another couple directly
   behind you facing the same way.
2. **Find your label.** The couple in front is the **First Couple**. The couple directly behind it —
   nearer the middle of the set — is the **Next Couple**.
3. **Every couple walks forward first**, still holding an ordinary
   [couple handhold](../02-language/hands-and-holds.md).
4. **Then turn where the caller says.** The First Couple arcs in the first direction given; the Next
   Couple — the one nearer the middle — has to walk an extra step or two forward to clear the couple
   in front before it starts its own arc, and then takes the second direction.
5. **Stop when you are facing the other dancers.** Sent in opposite directions, each couple turns
   through half a circle on a tight curve and you finish in **Facing Lines**.

The turn is a turn of the whole couple, not two separate turns: the dancer on the inside of the curve
is the pivot and the dancer on the outside walks the wider arc. That is the same
[Wheel Around](m08-wheel-around.md) action you already know.

### Timing

**6 beats** — three bars of music, which is a comfortable walking arc. The manual's timing is 6; the
animation runs in 4. There is a little room here, because the Next Couple has a longer path
than the First Couple: if the caller rushes, the two couples bunch up. See
[Timing and music](../01-start/timing-and-music.md).

### Styling

> "Use Couple handholds. Dancers in each Couple who are making the tighter turn serve as the pivot
> point and should exert slight pressure to assist as in any Wheel Around movement."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

Keep your couple handhold throughout; the inside dancer pivots, the outside dancer walks the wider
arc. If the caller sends both couples the same way, the manual says to "adjust to a Promenade
handhold" because the next call is normally a Promenade.

### Restrictions

The manual records one restriction, and it is about a variant rather than the main call:

> "The following sequence is occasionally used (women must be centers): Tag the Line (or Double Pass
> Thru); Lady Go Left, Gent Go Right, Allemande Left."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

So when the caller says "Lady Go Left, Gent Go Right", the two Ladies must be the center dancers —
otherwise the immediate Allemande Left cannot complete the sequence. The manual also asks callers not
to expand on this usage, and warns that these definitions "are not going to further define "Go"". See
[Mainstream restrictions](../03-rules/mainstream-restrictions.md).

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| The two couples collide at the start | The Next Couple turned too early | The Next Couple walks forward first, to clear the couple ahead, and only then turns |
| You and your partner turn as two separate people | Losing the couple handhold | Hold hands. One of you pivots, the other walks the wider arc |
| You finish a quarter turn short of facing the other line | Turning less than the half circle the manual asks for | Turn until you are facing the dancers in the other line, then stop |
| You cannot tell whether you are First or Next | Not looking at the formation before the call | First is the couple in front; Next is the couple behind it, nearer the middle |

### Where you meet it

This call is taught in [Class 7](../05-course/class-07.md), immediately after
[Double Pass Thru](m24-double-pass-thru.md). It is the standard way a caller turns the finished
Double Pass Thru into two facing lines, ready for a Ladies Chain, a Pass Thru or a Right and Left
Thru.

### Related calls

- **[Double Pass Thru](m24-double-pass-thru.md)** — the call you have just danced.
- **[Wheel Around](m08-wheel-around.md)** — the same pivot-and-walk turn, used by one couple.
- **[Promenade](m03-promenade-family.md)** — where the same-direction version ends.
- **[Tag the Line](m49-tag-the-line-family.md)** — the other call that finishes in this formation.

### Also called / formerly

The manual's full title is **"First Couple Go Left/Right, Next Couple Go Left/Right"**; this unit
uses the opposite-directions version in its title because that is the one you meet most. Callers
shorten it constantly: "First Couple Left, Next Right", "First Couple Go Left, Next Couple Go Right".
If both couples are sent the **same** way — "First Couple Go Left, Next Couple Go Left" — the ending
is a Promenade or Wrong Way Promenade instead of Facing Lines, and the next call is usually Promenade.
