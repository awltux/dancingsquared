# M29 — Dive Thru

> **Teaching unit M29** of the CALLERLAB (New) Mainstream Suggested Teaching Order, effective
> 1 September 2026. Prerequisites: [Facing Couples](../02-language/formations.md#facing-couples),
> [Normal Couple](../02-language/couples-and-positions.md),
> [California Twirl](m27-california-twirl.md).

## Dive Thru

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Facing Couples](../02-language/formations.md#facing-couples) — at least one of them Normal |
| **Ending formation** | [Tandem Couples](../02-language/formations.md#tandem) |
| **Timing** | 2 beats for the couple diving under, 6 for the couple making the arch |
| **Unit** | M29 of 50 |

### Dance action

> "One Couple makes an arch by raising their joined hands, while the other Couple ducks under the
> arch. Both move forward. The Couple making the arch does a California Twirl."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

The manual then says which couple does which, unless the caller tells you otherwise:

> "Unless otherwise specified, it is the Couple whose back is to the center of square who makes the
> arch, and the outside Couple who ducks under."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

Two couples are facing each other. One couple raises an arch; the other walks under it. Both couples
keep moving forwards.

1. **Work out which couple you are.** Unless the caller says otherwise, the couple whose back is to
   the middle of the square makes the arch, and the couple facing in from the outside ducks under.
2. **If you are the arch couple, raise your joined hands.** Take an ordinary
   [couple handhold](../02-language/hands-and-holds.md), lift it high, and stand far enough apart that
   a whole couple can walk through the gap.
3. **If you are the diving couple, duck.** Keep your couple handhold, bend low, and walk forward
   under the arch and out the other side.
4. **Both couples keep walking forward.** The diving couple comes out the far side. The arch couple
   immediately does a [California Twirl](m27-california-twirl.md) — same arch, same joined hands —
   and finishes facing the other way.
5. **Stop in [tandem couples](../02-language/formations.md#tandem).** Each couple is now one behind the
   other, and the two couples are back to back.

The manual is clear that the arch couple should "keep the arch, and blend smoothly into the California
Twirl, rather than dropping their hands and then raising them again" — it is one continuous movement,
not two.

### Timing

The manual gives **two numbers**, because the two couples do different amounts of work: **2 beats for
the couple diving under** and **6 for the couple making the arch**, who still have a California Twirl
to dance. Six beats is three bars. That is why Dive Thru feels unhurried for the divers and busy for
the arch: the divers are done in half a bar and the arch couple carry on. See
[Timing and music](../01-start/timing-and-music.md).

### Styling

> "The Couple making the arch uses a loose handhold. Stand far enough apart to allow another Couple to
> dive under. It is permissible for dancers making the arch to part hands momentarily if it is
> uncomfortable to reach over diving dancers."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

The diving couple uses an ordinary couple handhold and should "bend low enough and stay close enough
to Partner to move comfortably underneath the arch". Nobody needs to duck dramatically; a small dip
and a steady walk is enough.

### Restrictions

The manual restricts who may start, and who may make the arch:

> "Starting formation: Facing Couples (at least one of which is normal)"

and, in the comments, "The Couple making the arch must be a Normal Couple in order to be able to do
the California Twirl."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

So a Dive Thru is only proper when at least one of the two couples is a
[Normal Couple](../02-language/couples-and-positions.md), and the arch must be made by a Normal Couple
— because a [Sashayed couple](../02-language/couples-and-positions.md) cannot dance the California
Twirl at the end of it. The manual also requires the caller to say who makes the arch if neither
couple has its back to the centre of the set. See
[Mainstream restrictions](../03-rules/mainstream-restrictions.md).

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| The arch collapses just as the other couple arrives | Dropping hands too early | Hold the arch until the diving couple is clear, then go straight into the California Twirl |
| The diving couple bang their heads | Standing too close together, or arching too low | The arch couple stand further apart and lift the hands high |
| The arch couple forget the California Twirl | Thinking the call is over once the others are through | The arch couple's job is only half done: they still have to twirl |
| Nobody knows who dives | Not listening for the caller's designation | If the caller does not say, the couple with their back to the centre makes the arch |

### Where you meet it

Dive Thru is taught in [Class 8](../05-course/class-08.md), with
[Slide Thru](m28-slide-thru.md) and [Box the Gnat](m30-box-the-gnat.md). Almost always it comes out of
an Eight Chain Thru and finishes in the Double Pass Thru formation, which is exactly where
[First Couple Go Left](m25-first-couple-go.md) and [Wheel and Deal](m26-wheel-and-deal.md) want to
start.

### Also called / formerly

Dive Thru has always been called Dive Thru. What varies is how the caller names the two couples —
**"Heads Arch, Sides Dive Thru"**, **"Centers Arch, Dive Thru"**, **"Outsides Dive Thru"**,
**"Dive in to the Middle"**. All of them are the same call with the roles spelled out; the manual's
command examples list them all.
