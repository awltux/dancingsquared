# M30 — Box the Gnat

> **Teaching unit M30** of the CALLERLAB (New) Mainstream Suggested Teaching Order, effective
> 1 September 2026. Prerequisites: [Facing Dancers](../02-language/formations.md#facing-dancers),
> [Passing Rule](../03-rules/passing-rule.md), [Ocean Wave Rule](../03-rules/ocean-wave-rule.md).

## Box the Gnat

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Facing Dancers](../02-language/formations.md#facing-dancers) — one Boy and one Girl |
| **Ending formation** | [Facing Dancers](../02-language/formations.md#facing-dancers) |
| **Timing** | 4 beats |
| **Unit** | M30 of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "Dancers join and raise their right hands to make an arch; these hands remain connected throughout
> the call. In one smooth motion dancers Pass Thru and turn around:
>
> - The Woman turns left and goes under the arch.
> - The Man turns right and walks forward around the Woman."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

Box the Gnat is a pass and a turn with your right hands joined the whole way through.

1. **Join right hands** with the dancer facing you and **raise them** to make an arch, about eye
   level. Do not let go — the manual says these hands "remain connected throughout the call".
2. **Both walk forward**, passing each other.
3. **The Girl turns left and walks under the arch.**
4. **The Boy turns right and walks forward around her**, keeping hold of her right hand.
5. **Stop, still facing.** You are back where you started, but you are now facing the dancer who was
   in front of you — each of you is standing in the other's original spot.

The manual describes the finish precisely: "At the end of the call, each dancer will be standing in
the other's original position and they will again be facing." Your right hands are still joined at
the end, and the manual notes that "often the next call will begin with the joined right hands" — so
expect the caller to go straight on.

Think of it as a [Star Thru](m18-star-thru.md) that puts you back facing your partner, or as a
[Pass Thru](m07-pass-thru.md) with a right-hand arch you never let go of.

### Timing

**4 beats** — two bars of music. The whole call is one continuous movement: no pause between the pass
and the turn. Because your hands stay joined, the two of you are moving around each other rather than
past each other, and 4 beats is comfortable.

### Styling

> "The initial handhold in this call is like a handshake but less connected than palm-to-palm.
> Throughout the call the dancers smoothly adjust this connection so that they end with the same
> handhold. Hands should slide over one another easily while still providing some degree of security
> and stabilization."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

A light grip. The hands turn and slide a little as you go round; a tight grip will jam the turn. The
[Ocean Wave Rule](../03-rules/ocean-wave-rule.md) applies to Box the Gnat, and the manual warns that
from a [mini-wave](../02-language/formations.md#waves) the dancers "smoothly adjust handholds into the one
described above" and may need to "back up slightly" first, depending on the regional wave handhold.

### Restrictions

The manual gives the starting formation as **"Facing Dancers, one Man and one Woman"**. Two Boys
facing, or two Girls, is **not** Box the Gnat — the call needs the Boy-and-Girl pair so that one
dancer goes under the arch and the other goes round. If the square has you facing a dancer of the same
role, the caller will use a different call. See
[Mainstream restrictions](../03-rules/mainstream-restrictions.md).

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| You let go of the right hand halfway round | Reaching for the pass as if it were Pass Thru | The right hands stay joined from start to finish |
| You finish beside your partner instead of facing them | Turning too far | Box the Gnat is a half turn each way: you end standing where the other dancer started, facing them |
| You both turn the same way and tangle | Turning by habit rather than by role | The Girl turns **left** and goes under; the Boy turns **right** and goes round |
| Your hands jam up | Too firm a grip | Use a handshake, not a clamp — the manual calls it "less connected than palm-to-palm" |

### Where you meet it

Box the Gnat is taught in [Class 8](../05-course/class-08.md), the last of the three calls that teach
you how to change a facing pair into something else. It appears often in singing calls, where a
caller wants the two dancers back facing each other without losing the handhold, and it is the
standard way into an Allemande Left from a Right and Left Grand.

### Related calls

- **[Star Thru](m18-star-thru.md)** — the same arch and turn, but ending as a couple.
- **[Slide Thru](m28-slide-thru.md)** — pass and turn with no hands at all.
- **[Pass Thru](m07-pass-thru.md)** — the pass without the turn.
- **[California Twirl](m27-california-twirl.md)** — the same arch action used to turn a couple
  around.

### Also called / formerly

Box the Gnat has always been called Box the Gnat. The manual records one named variant you will
meet: **"Box the Gnat, Change Hands"**, in which the dancers change hands at the end — the manual's
own example is "Box the Gnat, Change Hands; Allemande Left". Older callers say Men and Women or Gents
and Ladies where this book says Boys and Girls.
