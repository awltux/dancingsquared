# M31 — Ocean Wave Family

> **Teaching unit M31** of the CALLERLAB (New) Mainstream Suggested Teaching Order, effective
> 1 September 2026. Prerequisites: [Ocean Wave](../02-language/formations.md#waves),
> [Facing Dancers](../02-language/formations.md#facing-dancers), [Dosado](m02-dosado.md).

The Ocean Wave Family is five related calls that all begin or end in a wave — the line of dancers
with alternating facing directions that most of square dancing is built on. You meet them together
because each one is a doorway into the same formation.

## a. Alamo Style

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | The [Allemande Left](m04-arm-turn-family.md) starting formation — a squared set, each dancer facing their corner |
| **Ending formation** | [Alamo Ring](../02-language/formations.md#alamo-and-thar) |
| **Timing** | 4 beats |
| **Unit** | M31a of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "Dancers start an Allemande Left but continue the Arm Turn until the men are looking toward the
> center of the square and the women are looking out. Maintain the left handhold and join right hands
> with the adjacent dancer to form an Alamo Ring."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

1. **Face your corner** — the dancer diagonally across the set from you — and take a left forearm
   hold, exactly as you would for an [Allemande Left](m04-arm-turn-family.md).
2. **Walk around each other by the left.** This time do not stop halfway.
3. **Keep going until the Men are facing the center** of the square and the Women are facing out.
   That is a little more than half a turn.
4. **Keep your left hand** where it is and reach out with your right hand to the dancer now beside
   you.

You finish in a ring of eight dancers with all adjacent hands joined and everybody alternately
facing in and out. That is an **Alamo Ring**.

### Timing

**4 beats** — two bars of music. It is the same timing as the Allemande Left it is built from,
which is why the two are usually called as one phrase.

### Styling

> "Bring both hands up at the same time, sliding smoothly out of the forearm grip of the Allemande
> Left, to blend into the same styling as Step to a Wave."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

Both hands arrive together. Do not finish the arm turn and *then* reach for the right hand — the two
handholds overlap.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| You stop in a normal Allemande Left and drop hands | The old habit took over | Keep turning past the half turn until you are looking in (Men) or out (Women) |
| The right-hand hold never forms and the ring has gaps | Reaching late | Bring both hands up at the same time as you finish the left turn |
| You end up back-to-back with your corner | You turned the wrong way | The turn is by the **left**, the same direction as an Allemande Left |
| The ring is lopsided | Some dancers took a shortcut through the middle | Walk a smooth circle; the left hold stays unbroken |

### Where you meet it

Alamo Style is almost always called as one phrase — **"Allemande Left in the Alamo Style"** — and it
is usually followed immediately by [Balance](#b-balance). See the
[course overview](../05-course/course-overview.md) for where this unit sits.

### Related calls

- **[Allemande Left](m04-arm-turn-family.md)** — the same turn, stopped halfway.
- **[Balance](#b-balance)** — the call that almost always follows it.
- **[Step to a Wave](#c-step-to-a-wave)** — the styling it blends into.

### Also called / formerly

The full command is **"Allemande Left in the Alamo Style"**. Many callers shorten it to **"Alamo
Style"** and expect you to know the rest. **"Right to Partner and Balance awhile"** is an added
instruction, not a different call.

## b. Balance

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Mini-Wave](../02-language/formations.md#waves) — usually inside an [Ocean Wave](../02-language/formations.md#waves) or an Alamo Ring |
| **Ending formation** | [Mini-Wave](../02-language/formations.md#waves) |
| **Timing** | 4 beats |
| **Unit** | M31b of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "Each dancer steps forward on one foot, using moderate tension in their connection with adjacent
> dancer(s), and pauses while bringing the other foot forward and touching it to the floor without
> transferring weight. Each dancer steps back on the free foot and pauses while touching the other
> foot beside it."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

1. **Keep every handhold you already have.** Do not let go.
2. **Step forward** on one foot — a short step, no farther than shoulder-to-shoulder — and lean
   gently against the hands you are holding.
3. **Touch your other foot** to the floor beside the first. Do not put your weight on it.
4. **Step back** on that free foot, and touch the other foot beside it.

That is one Balance: forward and back, four beats. It is a gentle rocking motion, not a lunge.

### Timing

**4 beats** — forward on the first two beats, back on the last two. Callers often call it as
"Balance, and Balance again" to fill eight beats.

### Styling

> "Dancers maintain handholds throughout the call."

> "Dancers should go forward no farther than shoulder-to-shoulder."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

The tension in your joined hands is what holds the wave together. Keep your arms springy and your
step small.

### Restrictions

The manual records that the [Facing Couples Rule](../03-rules/facing-couples-rule.md) does **not**
apply to Balance. If the caller wants a Balance, you must already be in a wave or a ring — the rule
that normally lets dancers add a [Step to a Wave](#c-step-to-a-wave) to reach a wave does not rescue
this one.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| You step a long stride forward | Treating it as a walk | No farther than shoulder-to-shoulder; the step is short |
| The wave pulls apart or collapses | Letting go, or pulling hard | Keep the handholds and use *moderate* tension — a spring, not a tug of war |
| You put your weight on the touching foot | The foot came down flat and heavy | Touch the toe, then step back with it — the weight never transfers |
| You finish facing a different way | Adding a turn | Balance has no turn in it at all; you end exactly as you started |

### Where you meet it

Balance is the standard way to mark time inside a wave. It is called from an Alamo Ring and from
Ocean Waves, most often as "Allemande Left in the Alamo Style, and Balance". See the
[course overview](../05-course/course-overview.md) for where this unit sits.

### Related calls

- **Alamo Style** — the call that usually precedes it. See [M31a](#a-alamo-style).
- **Step to a Wave** — how you get into the wave you are balancing in. See [M31c](#c-step-to-a-wave).
- **Swing Thru** — the other thing callers do from a wave. See [M33](m33-swing-thru.md).

### Also called / formerly

You will hear **"Balance forward and back"**, **"Balance up and back"**, and **"Balance awhile"**.
They all mean this call.

## c. Step to a Wave

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Facing Dancers](../02-language/formations.md#facing-dancers) |
| **Ending formation** | Right-Hand [Mini-Wave](../02-language/formations.md#waves) |
| **Timing** | 2 beats |
| **Unit** | M31c of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "Dancers walk forward a small step and join right hands."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

1. Stand facing another dancer, a normal step apart.
2. **Both step forward** — one small step is enough.
3. **Join right hands** with that dancer as you arrive.

You finish standing right-hand-to-right-hand, still facing each other across the joined hands. That
is a **Mini-Wave** — and when four of them stand in a line, an Ocean Wave.

### Timing

**2 beats** — one step forward, one to settle into the handhold. It is the shortest call in this
family.

### Styling

> "Dancers should finish using Hands Up position with all adjacent dancers."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

"Hands Up" means the joined hands are held up at about chest or shoulder height, elbows relaxed and
down, palms joined with light pressure — the same hold you use in every wave. Do not lock your
elbow or lean back on the handhold.

### Restrictions

The manual records two things here. The [Ocean Wave Rule](../03-rules/ocean-wave-rule.md) does
**not** apply to Step to a Wave. And the [Facing Couples Rule](../03-rules/facing-couples-rule.md)
requires certain dancers to add Step to a Wave before some other calls — which is why you will often
hear it inside a longer call rather than on its own.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| You step forward and stop with hands at your sides | Forgetting the handhold | The handhold *is* the call — join right hands as your foot lands |
| Your left hand also comes up into a wave hold with nobody there | Reaching for the wave you expect to be in | Join the **right** hand only; there is nobody on your left yet |
| You stand too far apart, arms straight | Taking too short a step | Take one honest step forward, then bend your elbows |
| You turn to face your partner instead of holding the wave | Thinking it is a couple call | You stay facing; a Mini-Wave is two dancers facing each other |

### Where you meet it

Step to a Wave is the standard way to turn a facing pair into a wave, and it is the last third of
[Pass the Ocean](m35-pass-the-ocean.md). See the
[course overview](../05-course/course-overview.md) for where this unit sits.

### Related calls

- **Dosado to a Wave** — the same finish after a Dosado. See [M31d](#d-dosado-to-a-wave).
- **Single Circle to a Wave** — the same finish after a circle. See [M31e](#e-single-circle-to-a-wave).
- **Pass the Ocean** — a three-part call that ends here. See [M35](m35-pass-the-ocean.md).
- **Pass Thru / Step Thru** — the same step forward with no handhold. See [M07](m07-pass-thru.md).

### Also called / formerly

**"Step to an Ocean Wave"** and **"Make a Wave"** both mean Step to a Wave. When the caller wants the
other hand, you will hear **"Step to a Left-Hand Wave"**.

## d. Dosado to a Wave

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Facing Dancers](../02-language/formations.md#facing-dancers) |
| **Ending formation** | Right-Hand [Mini-Wave](../02-language/formations.md#waves) |
| **Timing** | 6 beats |
| **Unit** | M31d of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "In one smooth motion, Dosado and Step to a Wave."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

1. **Start a [Dosado](m02-dosado.md)** exactly as usual: walk forward, pass right shoulders, step to
   the right past each other, and back up around into place.
2. **Do not stop when you get back to your spot.** In the same movement, take one more small step
   forward.
3. **Join right hands** with the dancer you just dosadoed.

Three moves — Dosado, step forward, right hand — but danced as one continuous journey. If you can
do a smooth Dosado you can do this.

### Timing

**6 beats** — 4 for the Dosado and 2 for the Step to a Wave, exactly as the two calls add up.

### Styling

> "Follow the styling for Dosado (page 29) and for Step to a Wave (item c. above)"

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

In plain terms: normal Dosado arm and body styling, finishing with the **Hands Up** wave position
from [Step to a Wave](#c-step-to-a-wave).

### Restrictions

The manual records that the [Ocean Wave Rule](../03-rules/ocean-wave-rule.md) **does** apply to this
call. That matters when you start from a Mini-Wave rather than a facing pair: the rule lets the call
begin from that wave instead of insisting you first "un-wave" into facing dancers.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| You finish the Dosado back on your spot and stand there | The old Dosado habit | Keep walking: the last step forward is part of the call |
| You stop with the handhold half made | Reaching too late | Start bringing the right hand up as you back out of the Dosado |
| The wave is too wide or too narrow | Backing up too far | Finish the Dosado close, then step in — the whole call is continuous |
| You dosado a different dancer than the one you take hands with | Losing orientation during the turn | Take the right hand of the dancer you started facing |

### Where you meet it

Because it is a Dosado and a Step to a Wave in one, Dosado to a Wave appears wherever callers want
to build a wave out of a facing pair without the extra command. It shows up in singing calls and in
"Allemande Left in Alamo Style, Dosado to a Wave". See the
[course overview](../05-course/course-overview.md) for where this unit sits.

### Related calls

- **Dosado** — the first two thirds of it. See [M02](m02-dosado.md).
- **Step to a Wave** — the last third. See [M31c](#c-step-to-a-wave).
- **Swing Thru** — a common call right after it. See [M33](m33-swing-thru.md).

### Also called / formerly

**"Dosado and make a Wave"** is the other common wording. Both are the same call.

## e. Single Circle to a Wave

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Facing Dancers](../02-language/formations.md#facing-dancers) |
| **Ending formation** | Right-Hand [Mini-Wave](../02-language/formations.md#waves) |
| **Timing** | 4 beats |
| **Unit** | M31e of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "Facing Dancers join both hands with each other and Circle Left halfway. Without stopping, they
> drop hands with each other and individually Veer Left, blending into a Right-Hand Mini-Wave."

> "If the caller directs 'Single Circle 3/4 to a Wave', facing dancers join both hands with each
> other and Circle Left 3/4, then continue to execute the rest of the call as above."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

1. **Face another dancer and join both hands** — your right in their left, your left in their right.
2. **Circle Left** together, halfway around. You have each swapped places, still holding both hands.
3. **Without stopping, let go** of both hands with each other.
4. **Veer Left** — that is, step forward and to your left, each of you doing it individually — and
   blend into a **right-hand hold** as you arrive.

You finish in a Mini-Wave facing the dancer you started with, right hands joined. If the caller says
"Single Circle 3/4 to a Wave", just circle three-quarters of the way around before letting go, then
do the veer exactly the same way.

### Timing

**4 beats** — two beats of circling, two beats of veer and handhold. The 3/4 version takes a little
longer in practice, and callers allow for it.

### Styling

> "In circling, the two dancers are slightly offset to the left so that their dance partner in the
> circle is more to their right than in front of them. Use smooth dance step, not buzz. When joining
> hands for circle, gents turn palms up, ladies palms down. Arms are bent comfortably at the elbows.
> Exert a small amount of pressure while circling. Blend into a Hands-up Ocean Wave position."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

The offset is what makes the veer work. Stand a little to the left of your partner as you circle, so
that when you let go your momentum already carries you left.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| You circle a full turn | Halfway felt like too little | Halfway is exactly to the opposite spot; the veer finishes the job |
| You stop dead before veering | Treating it as two calls | "Without stopping" is in the definition — keep the feet moving |
| You veer right instead of left | Muscle memory from other calls | The veer is always **left**, whichever hand you finish holding |
| You end up facing out of the set | Veering too far past the wave | The veer is a small step forward and to the left, then stop |

### Where you meet it

Single Circle to a Wave is a compact way to build a wave without a Dosado or a Pass the Ocean, and it
appears mostly in singing calls. The 3/4 version is the more common of the two. See the
[course overview](../05-course/course-overview.md) for where this unit sits.

### Related calls

- **Step to a Wave** — the plain version of the same finish. See [M31c](#c-step-to-a-wave).
- **Veer Left / Veer Right** — the second half of this call on its own. See
  [M17](m17-veer-left-veer-right.md).
- **Circle Left/Right** — the first half on its own. See [M01](m01-circle-family.md).
- **Swing Thru** — a common call from the wave you finish in. See [M33](m33-swing-thru.md).

### Also called / formerly

The call is normally given in full: **"Single Circle to a Wave"**. The fraction, as in **"Single
Circle 3/4 to a Wave"**, is the only variation.
