# M33 — Swing Thru / Left Swing Thru

> **Teaching unit M33** of the CALLERLAB (New) Mainstream Suggested Teaching Order, effective
> 1 September 2026. Prerequisites: [Ocean Wave](../02-language/formations.md#waves),
> [Alamo Ring](../02-language/formations.md#alamo-and-thar), [Ocean Wave Rule](../03-rules/ocean-wave-rule.md).

## Swing Thru / Left Swing Thru

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Ocean Wave](../02-language/formations.md#waves) or [Alamo Ring](../02-language/formations.md#alamo-and-thar) |
| **Ending formation** | [Ocean Wave](../02-language/formations.md#waves) or [Alamo Ring](../02-language/formations.md#alamo-and-thar) |
| **Timing** | 6 beats |
| **Unit** | M33 of 50 |

### Dance action

> "Swing Thru: Those who can turn 1/2 (180 degrees) by the right; then those who can turn 1/2
> (180 degrees) by the left."

> "Left Swing Thru: Those who can turn 1/2 (180 degrees) by the left; then those who can turn 1/2
> (180 degrees) by the right."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

1. **Look at the dancers next to you.** In the wave, one of your two neighbours is holding your
   right hand and the other is holding your left hand.
2. **Those who can turn by the right** — that is, dancers with a **right** hand joined to somebody —
   [Arm Turn](m04-arm-turn-family.md) half way (180 degrees) with that neighbour.
3. **Then those who can turn by the left** — dancers who now have a **left** hand joined — Arm Turn
   half way with that neighbour.

The "those who can" wording is the whole trick: you only ever turn with a hand you are actually
holding. In a wave of four, the two dancers on the ends do one turn each and the two in the middle do
the other one. Everyone moves once.

For **Left Swing Thru**, swap the hands: the left-hand turn comes first, then the right.

You finish in a wave, but with everybody's facing direction reversed.

### Timing

**6 beats** — three beats for each half turn. The two halves run straight into each other with no
pause, so it feels like one flowing movement.

### Styling

> "Use Hands Up throughout the call. The first part of the call blends smoothly into the second
> part."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

Do not drop your arms between the two turns. The end of the first arm turn *is* the start of the
second, and the wave handhold stays up the whole time.

### Restrictions

The manual is explicit about where this call may begin. After applying the
[Facing Couples Rule](../03-rules/facing-couples-rule.md), **the starting formation of Swing Thru
must be a Wave or an Alamo Ring**; it is improper, for example, to call "Swing Thru" from an Inverted
Line.

The Plus program manual records the same limit in a slightly different way: at Mainstream, **Swing
Thru and Left Swing Thru may start only from a [Wave](../02-language/formations.md#waves) of at least three
dancers, or from an [Alamo Ring](../02-language/formations.md#alamo-and-thar)**. A two-dancer Mini-Wave on its own
is not enough; you need at least a wave of three.

There is one extended application. Dancers may work in other groups **provided** that, after the
Facing Couples Rule is applied, they are in a Wave of three or more dancers — and the caller **must**
clearly say which dancers are working together, as in "In the Wave of 6, Swing Thru". Eight dancers
working as one group is very unusual and reserved for programs where Grand Swing Thru exists.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Everybody tries to turn at once | Missing the "those who can" words | Only the dancers with that hand joined turn; if you are not holding it, wait |
| The wave collapses between the two halves | Dropping the arms | Keep Hands Up; the first turn flows straight into the second |
| You end facing the way you started | Turning a full turn | Each turn is a half turn (180 degrees), not 360 |
| You turn with the wrong neighbour | Reaching for a hand that is not joined | Turn only with the hand that is actually holding yours |

### Where you meet it

Swing Thru is one of the most used calls in the whole Mainstream program. It follows
[Pass the Ocean](m35-pass-the-ocean.md), [Step to a Wave](m31-ocean-wave-family.md) and almost any
call that ends in a wave, and it is everywhere in singing calls. If you can dance Swing Thru without
thinking, you can follow a caller through most of an evening. See the
[course overview](../05-course/course-overview.md) for where this unit sits.

### Also called / formerly

The reverse-handed version is always called in full as **"Left Swing Thru"**. The manual also notes
that from a Tidal Wave, Swing Thru is danced in the wave on each half — no dancer crosses the center
of the Tidal Wave.
