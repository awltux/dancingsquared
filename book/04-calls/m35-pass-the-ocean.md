# M35 — Pass the Ocean

> **Teaching unit M35** of the CALLERLAB (New) Mainstream Suggested Teaching Order, effective
> 1 September 2026. Prerequisites: [Facing Couples](../02-language/formations.md#facing-couples),
> [Pass Thru](m07-pass-thru.md), [Step to a Wave](m31-ocean-wave-family.md),
> [Ocean Wave Rule](../03-rules/ocean-wave-rule.md).

## Pass the Ocean

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting formation** | [Facing Couples](../02-language/formations.md#facing-couples) **only** at Mainstream |
| **Ending formation** | Right-Hand [Ocean Wave](../02-language/formations.md#waves) |
| **Timing** | 4 beats |
| **Unit** | M35 of 50 |

### Animation

▶ **Play this call** — pause, step and scrub it: [Watching the animations](../08-reference/watching-the-animations.md).

### Dance action

> "Pass Thru; Face your Partner; Step to a Wave"

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

### What you do

You start facing your partner, with another couple alongside you.

1. **Pass Thru.** Walk forward past the dancer you are facing, passing right shoulders, and stop
   [back-to-back](../02-language/formations.md#back-to-back-couples).
2. **Face your partner.** Turn where you stand — a quarter turn — to look at the dancer you just
   passed. You are now facing each other again, and the other couple has done the same.
3. **Step to a Wave.** Take one small step forward and join **right hands** with your partner.

You finish in a right-hand Ocean Wave: four dancers in a line, right hands joined in the middle, left
hands reaching out to the ends of the wave.

### Timing

**4 beats** — roughly one beat of passing, one of turning and two of stepping into the wave. Callers
put a Pass the Ocean on the same phrase as a [Swing Thru](m33-swing-thru.md).

### Styling

> "Although this is a 3-part definition, it is frequently danced in one smooth action. However, none
> of the dancers are considered to have a turning body flow at the end of this call."

— CALLERLAB, *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions*, revised 29 March 2026.

Dance it as one movement rather than three, but do not spin or add momentum on the last step. The
turn to face is a plain quarter turn.

### Restrictions

The manual is blunt about the starting formation. Pass the Ocean at Mainstream is **"Restricted at
Mainstream to Facing Couples only"** — you must begin as a couple facing another couple. The same
restriction is recorded in the Plus program manual, which notes that at Mainstream Pass the Ocean may
start **only from [Facing Couples](../02-language/formations.md#facing-couples)**.

The manual also records that **the application of the [Ocean Wave Rule](../03-rules/ocean-wave-rule.md)
to this call is not used at Mainstream**, although it may be applied in other programs. So do not
expect the call to be legal from a wave or a line.

### Common faults

| What happens | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| You stop back-to-back and never turn | Forgetting the middle part | After the Pass Thru you turn a quarter to face your partner |
| You step into the wave still facing out | Skipping the quarter turn | Face first, *then* step forward and take the right hand |
| The wave is left-handed | Taking the wrong hand | At Mainstream it is always a **right**-hand wave |
| You swing past your partner and end up somewhere else | Adding spin on the turn | It is a traffic-free quarter turn — no body flow at the end |

### Where you meet it

Pass the Ocean is the standard way to build an Ocean Wave from facing couples, and it is the gateway
to a huge amount of Mainstream dancing. Almost every tip will have one, usually followed by
[Swing Thru](m33-swing-thru.md), [Boys Trade](m32-trade-family.md) or
[Boys Run](m34-run-cross-run.md). See the
[course overview](../05-course/course-overview.md) for where this unit sits.

### Also called / formerly

Pass the Ocean has always been called **Pass the Ocean**. The manual notes that although the styling
encourages one smooth movement, the definition has three distinct parts, and in other programs a
caller may use those parts separately — for example "Pass the Ocean, all but the last part".
