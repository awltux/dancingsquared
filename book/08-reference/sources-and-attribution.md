# Sources and attribution

Every definition, timing and styling note in this book comes from CALLERLAB — the international
association of square dance callers — and nothing else.

## The two documents

Both are published by CALLERLAB Inc., The International Association of Square Dance Callers.

| Document | Revision | What it is used for |
|---|---|---|
| *Mainstream Dance Program Definitions* | **29 March 2026** | what is in the program, the order it is taught in, and each call's starting formation, dance action, ending formation, timing, styling and restrictions |
| *Plus Program Definitions* | **25 August 2026** | the [Plus appendix](../07-plus/where-mainstream-leads.md), and what Plus changes about Mainstream |

The Mainstream document is the one that matters most: **it decides what this book teaches.** It is the
document that replaced the old Basic, Mainstream and SSD lists with **50 calls or call families**
following the 2025 membership vote, and it carries the teaching order — the collection `M1` … `M50`,
effective **1 September 2026**.

Where an older habit, or a phrase you have heard in a hall, disagrees with the document, the document
wins. Usually the call page tells you both.

## Where the quotations come from

Passages in quotation marks, attributed to CALLERLAB, are the words of those two documents. They are
reproduced here under the permission below, and they are the only text in this book that is not
written for it. Everything else — the explanations, the faults, the drills, the course — is written
for a dancer starting out.

## The animations

The animations and the small formation diagrams show where each dancer goes, worked out from the
positions and facings the calls are defined with. They are a picture of the definition — useful for
checking where you should be standing and which way you should end up facing — and they are not a
substitute for a caller, a teacher, or the styling notes on each call page.

If an animation and a definition ever appear to disagree, **believe the definition.** The few calls
with no animation are listed in the [animations index](animations-index.md).

## A caution about names

The definitions are CALLERLAB's, but the names you hear in a hall are the dancers'. Where a call has
older or regional names, the call page lists them under **Also called / formerly**, so that the word
someone sings out at you and the word in this book can be recognised as the same thing.

## The callers' shorthand

[Sequence and relationship](../02-language/sequence-and-relationship.md) uses the framework callers
work in — **F**ormation, **A**rrangement, **S**equence, **R**elationship — and the relationship
letters **p**, **c**, **o** and **r**. That is callers' terminology rather than part of the definitions
document, and it follows **Rich Reel's "FASR and other Caller Terminology"** on all8.com
([fasr.htm](https://www.all8.com/sd/calling/fasr.htm)), whose arrangement tables also supply the six
squared-set arrangements drawn on
[Arrangements and gender](../02-language/arrangements-and-gender.md). Credit for that material is
Rich Reel's; where it and the definitions document disagree about a call, the document still wins.
