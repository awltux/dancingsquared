# Watching the animations

Every call in this book that can be shown moving has a **player** on its page: a picture of the floor
seen from above, with a bar of controls under it.

## The controls

There is **one animation per call**, and it is this one — nothing is repeated further down the page.

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| **⏮** | jump to the **starting setup** — where everyone stands before the call |
| **⏪** | step back a quarter of a beat |
| **▶ / ⏸** | play, and pause |
| **⏩** | step forward a quarter of a beat |
| **⏭** | jump to the **ending setup** — where everyone should finish |

Some calls also carry a **start** chooser, on its own line under the buttons. Most calls start from one
formation, so most pages have no chooser at all. Where the manual gives several, the chooser lists
them and the call is re-drawn from the one you pick — the dancers start standing differently, and a
call that travels further is drawn a little smaller to fit. One or two calls are shown from a
formation the manual does not list for them, under the name that arrangement goes by here.

Under that is a **call** chooser, which lists the other calls in this book that begin from the
formation you are looking at — so from a circle you can find everything else that starts from a
circle, without going back to the index. The two choosers rebuild each other: change the formation and
the list of calls is redrawn for it, change the call and the formation list becomes that call's own.
While the player is showing a call from another page, a line under it names that call and offers two
ways out — that call's own page, or back to the call this page is about. The list holds what this book
can animate from that formation; 75 calls are in the catalogue altogether.

Between the buttons is a **slider** carrying the whole call. Drag it and the dancers move with your
hand: the picture is worked out for the exact point you have dragged to, so there is no smearing and
no guessing what happens between frames. That is the point of the whole thing — if you lose your place
in a class, you can drag back until the picture matches what you remember, and see precisely where you
went wrong.

At the right is a **speed** choice: **1/4**, **1/2** or **full**. The player starts at **half speed**,
which is close to a caller walking a call through. Full speed is the tempo you will meet it at in a
hall — 128 beats a minute — and it is worth watching a call at full speed once you think you know it,
because that is the only way to find out whether you really do.

> **A tip that saves time.** Learn a call from the **ending setup** backwards. Press **⏭**, look at
> where everyone ends up, then step back a beat at a time with **⏪**. Dancers who know the finish
> rarely get lost in the middle.

## The floor

The floor, its grid and what the spacing on it means are set out on [Conventions](../conventions.md),
which is the page for reading any picture in this book. One thing belongs here: the small
[formation diagrams](../02-language/formations.md) are drawn on the same grid as the player, so a
formation there and the same formation mid-call are drawn to the same measure.

## The colours

The dancers are drawn as two shapes, so that nothing depends on colour alone:

| Shape | Dancer |
|---|---|
| **circle** | a **Girl** — the dancer who squared up on the right of a couple |
| **square** | a **Boy** — the dancer who squared up on the left of a couple |

The **dark wedge** on each dancer is the direction that dancer is **facing** — read it as a beak, and
the dancer is looking that way. It is drawn in near-black rather than in the dancer's own colour so
that it reads at a glance against both the coloured body and the pale floor. Where two dancers come
very close, a wedge can be hidden behind the neighbour it points at; that is the picture being honest
about one dancer being in front of another. A thick grey line between two dancers is a **joined
handhold**, drawn only across the gap between the two bodies, so it never reads as a pair of very long
arms. The faint coloured trail behind a dancer is where that dancer has already been during this call.

The colour of a dancer is the **couple they started in**:

| Colour | Couple |
|---|---|
| **red** | couple 1 |
| **blue** | couple 2 |
| **green** | couple 3 |
| **gold** | couple 4 |

## The numbers

A number inside a dancer is that dancer's **home position** in the square, which is also the number
you will hear called at you:

| Position | Dancer |
|---|---|
| **1** | the Boy of the **south** couple (the couple nearest you, as you look at the picture) |
| **2** | the Girl of the south couple |
| **3** | the Boy of the **west** couple |
| **4** | the Girl of the west couple |
| **5** | the Boy of the **north** couple |
| **6** | the Girl of the north couple |
| **7** | the Boy of the **east** couple |
| **8** | the Girl of the east couple |

So numbers run **anticlockwise** from the south couple, and the south couple is **1 and 2**. Every
"Number 3" or "the number eights" you hear from a caller refers to this table.

Numbers appear **only when the call begins from a full squared set**. Where it begins from anything
smaller — half a square, facing couples, a single wave — there is no home position to number, so no
number is shown, and the colours fall back to gender. That is not a missing feature: a diagram that
numbered half a square would be telling you something that is not true. The [conventions
page](../conventions.md) explains the rule.

## If the player does not appear

The player needs the site to be served over **http or https**, not opened as a file from your own
disk. Open from the site address and it will run; open the raw file and the book will tell you so
rather than showing you a blank box.

## Taking it with you

The site can be **installed** like an app — from your browser's menu, choose *Install* or *Add to Home
Screen*. Once installed it opens in its own window, works on a phone or a tablet, and keeps working
**without a signal**: the whole book, and every call you can watch, are stored on the device. That is
the version to take to a class, or to a dance weekend with unreliable wifi.
