# Conventions — how this book is laid out

Read this once and every page afterwards looks familiar.

## One definition, one place

Every term is **defined once**, on the page named in the [glossary](08-reference/glossary.md), and
**linked** everywhere else. Call pages do not re-explain a concept; they link to it. That is why a
call page can be 40 lines and still make sense on its own — the words it uses are one click away.

## The call card

Every call follows the same shape, so you always know where to look:

| Section | What it gives you |
|---|---|
| **At a glance** | starting formation → ending formation, timing in beats, and the teaching unit |
| **Animation** | the call danced, from its starting formation to its ending one |
| **What you do** | plain-language instruction, in the second person |
| **Timing** | the beats, and what that means in music |
| **Styling** | hands, arms, posture — how it should *look* |
| **Restrictions** | where the manual says the call may **not** start, or what it may not follow |
| **Common faults** | what beginners get wrong, and the fix |
| **Where you meet it** | the classes that teach it |
| **Related calls** | links, never repetitions |
| **Also called / formerly** | the other words you will hear in a hall |

A call with no animation says **why** instead — see below.

## Reading the animations

Every animation is drawn **looking straight down at the floor**, with the walls of the square square
to the page: the bottom of the picture is the side of the square **nearest you**, and the top is the
far side. The faint grid behind the dancers is the floor, drawn **one unit per square**, and **a
dancer is one square wide** — so the spacing between dancers can be read off the grid rather than
guessed. Dancers stand two squares apart in a squared set or a wave, and one square apart in a tidal
wave or a promenade.

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **circle** | a Girl (the dancer who squared up on the right of a couple) |
| **square** | a Boy (the dancer who squared up on the left of a couple) |
| **the dark wedge on each marker** | which way that dancer is **facing** — drawn near-black so it reads at a glance |
| **thick grey line** | a joined handhold between two dancers |
| **coloured trail** | where that dancer has travelled so far; the trail grows as the call is danced |
| **number inside a marker** | the dancer's home position (1–8), when the call begins from a full squared set |

**Shape and facing are always drawn**, so nothing depends on colour alone.

**Colour** is by **couple** — 1 red, 2 blue, 3 green, 4 gold — whenever the call begins from a full
set of eight, so you can see at a glance who belongs with whom. Many calls are defined for only *half*
the set: Star Thru from Facing Couples involves four dancers, and there is no home couple to point at.
Then colour falls back to **gender** and no numbers are shown. The picture is still correct; it simply
does not claim to know something it cannot know. Guessing a couple number from where somebody happens
to be standing would be inventing identity, and that is exactly what a diagram must never do.

## When a call has no animation

Some calls in the 2026 program have **no animation yet** — there is nothing to draw them from — and
those are taught from the definition alone. Each of them says so rather than leaving a blank space,
and the [animations index](08-reference/animations-index.md) lists them together, so you can always
tell a missing animation apart from a missing you.

An animation is also shown **only where it agrees with the 2026 definitions**. A moving picture of an
*older* version of a call is worse than no picture at all, so where the two disagree the animation is
withheld and the page says so.

## Quoting the manual

Text in quotation marks attributed to the manual is CALLERLAB's own wording, reproduced under the
permission printed in [sources and attribution](08-reference/sources-and-attribution.md). Everything
else is written for this book.

## Words this book uses deliberately

The 2026 manual says **Boys and Girls**. Older material, and many callers, say *Men and Women*,
*Gents and Ladies*. Both are listed on each call's "also called" line, because you will hear both.
The book teaches the 2026 word.
