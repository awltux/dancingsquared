# Introduction

This book teaches **Mainstream square dancing** to somebody who has never danced it. It assumes no
earlier dancing, no teacher at your elbow, and not one word of the language — the words are taught in
[the language](02-language/naming-the-dancers.md), and everything after that is built out of them.

## What is in it

Every call in the **CALLERLAB Mainstream Dance Program** as it stands in 2026, each on its own page:
the formation it starts from and the one it ends in, the timing, what you actually do, how it should
look, and what beginners usually get wrong. The program took effect on **1 September 2026**.

Most of the calls are shown **moving**, with controls to pause, step and scrub the movement, because
where eight dancers end up is not the same thing as watching the eight of them get there. A few have
no animation, and those pages say so rather than leaving a blank space — the
[animations index](08-reference/animations-index.md) lists them together.

The program itself changed in 2026: the old Basic, Mainstream and SSD levels were replaced by a
single Mainstream program of **fifty calls and call families**. If you learned to dance before that,
[moved and renamed](08-reference/moved-and-renamed.md) says what changed; if you have never danced,
nothing on that page concerns you yet.

## Three ways to read it

- **From the beginning, in order.** [What square dancing is](01-start/what-is-square-dancing.md) →
  [The square](01-start/the-square.md) → [Naming the dancers](02-language/naming-the-dancers.md), then
  the classes in [the course](05-course/course-overview.md), with
  [the calls](04-calls/m01-circle-family.md) as the reference beside you. This is the path a class
  follows.
- **One call at a time.** [The call index](08-reference/call-index.md) lists every call. Each page
  stands on its own and links to the pages that explain the words it uses.
- **To teach.** [Teaching a class](05-course/teaching-a-class.md), with the
  [drills](06-practice/drills.md).

Any word you meet and do not know is in the [glossary](08-reference/glossary.md), which points at the
one page that defines it.

## What it is not

It is not a substitute for a caller. Nobody learns to dance from a book: the book tells you what the
calls are and what to do, and a caller and seven other dancers turn that into dancing.

It is not the rulebook. Where this book quotes the manual, the quotation is marked and the page is
given; the definitions themselves are CALLERLAB's, reproduced under the permission printed in
[sources and attribution](08-reference/sources-and-attribution.md), which says which manual, which
revision, and under what terms.

And it is only Mainstream. What comes after it is in
[where Mainstream leads](07-plus/where-mainstream-leads.md).

> [!WARNING]
> **This book was written by an AI, so it may contain mistakes and hallucinations.** Every page was
> generated from CALLERLAB's Mainstream definitions by a language model, and a language model states
> things confidently whether or not they are true — an invented timing, a formation the call never
> reaches, a fault nobody has ever seen. The definitions are the authority: where this book and the
> manual disagree, **the manual is right**, and the call pages quote it so that you can compare. The
> book's sources are described in
> [sources and attribution](08-reference/sources-and-attribution.md).
>
> **The animations are drawn by an experimental engine.** It is a square dance engine still under test,
> working from **Taminations** call data. Because Taminations does not author every call this book
> teaches, that data carries extensions for the rest — and those extended calls do not all work as
> expected yet, so an animation can be wrong while looking entirely plausible. The **handholds** are the
> worst of it: which hand is holding which is what the engine gets wrong most often, so a picture can
> join two dancers who are not touching, or join the wrong hands. Where an animation and the definition
> disagree, **believe the definition**.
>
> **Corrections are always welcome.** If something here is wrong,
> [log in to GitHub and raise an issue, mentioning 'book_build_hash={{build-hash}}'](https://github.com/awltux/dancingsquared/issues).
